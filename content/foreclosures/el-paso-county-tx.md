# El Paso County Foreclosure Auctions: Trustee Sales & Tax Sales Guide (2026)

El Paso County runs two completely separate foreclosure auction systems — and most investors only know about one. Mortgage-backed properties go to a monthly courthouse auction held on the first Tuesday of every month under Texas non-judicial foreclosure law. Tax-delinquent properties flow through a separate online platform managed by the El Paso County Sheriff's Office. Each system has its own operator, its own deed type, its own redemption rules, and its own platform. If you're searching for "el paso county foreclosure auction," understanding which track you're on is the first decision you'll make.

This guide covers both tracks — where to find the listings, how each auction works, what you'll pay, and the El Paso-specific market factors that set this border county apart from the rest of Texas.

---

## Two Tracks: How El Paso County Foreclosures Work

**Track 1 — Non-Judicial Trustee Sales** are the traditional mortgage foreclosures. When a homeowner defaults on a loan secured by a deed of trust, the lender's appointed trustee files a Trustee's Notice of Sale with the El Paso County Clerk's Office and schedules the property for auction on the next available first Tuesday. These auctions are governed by **Texas Property Code Chapter 51** and take place at the El Paso County Courthouse. No court involvement is required.

**Track 2 — Sheriff's Tax Foreclosure Sales** are ordered by a court when a property owner fails to pay property taxes. These sales are conducted by the El Paso County Sheriff's Office through an online auction platform at **elpaso.texas.sheriffsaleauctions.com**. Tax sale listings are posted on the 15th of each month. Registration is required through the Realauction.com platform before bidding.

These two tracks involve different properties, different platforms, different deed types, different redemption rights, and different risks. The sections below break each one down.

> **Quick note on El Paso County, Colorado:** If you've been landing on `elpasopublictrustee.com` or `elpasoco.realforeclose.com`, those are for El Paso County, Colorado (Colorado Springs area), which uses Colorado's Public Trustee system. Texas El Paso County's official sites are `epcountytx.gov` and `epcounty.com`.

## Track 1: Non-Judicial Trustee Sales (Mortgage Foreclosures)

### How the Texas Non-Judicial Process Works

Texas is a non-judicial foreclosure state. When a borrower defaults on a mortgage secured by a deed of trust, the lender can foreclose without going to court. The trustee named in the deed of trust — or a substitute trustee appointed by the lender — manages the sale process:

1. **Default** — Borrower misses payments and exhausts any cure period in the loan agreement
2. **Notice of Trustee's Sale** — The trustee files a formal Notice of Trustee's Sale with the El Paso County Clerk's Office and mails written notice to the borrower at least **21 days** before the auction date
3. **Notice publication** — Notice is posted at the courthouse and in the county (may also be published in a local newspaper)
4. **First-Tuesday auction** — Property is auctioned at the El Paso County Courthouse on the first Tuesday of the month, between 10:00 AM and 4:00 PM
5. **Sale and deed** — Winning bidder pays in full at the auction; the trustee delivers a Substitute Trustee's Deed

**There is no right of redemption for mortgage foreclosures in Texas.** Title transfers at the gavel. The buyer takes possession of the property after the sale and deed delivery — the former owner has no statutory right to reclaim the property.

### Finding the List

The El Paso County Clerk's Office maintains the official foreclosure filing database:

- **Online search portal:** apps.epcountytx.gov/publicrecords/Foreclosures — searchable by sale date, instrument number, subdivision, lot, and block
- **How-to guide (PDF):** Available at the portal for first-time searchers
- **Office location:** 500 E. San Antonio Ave, Room 105, El Paso, TX 79901
- **Phone:** (915) 546-2071

Search by sale date to pull all properties scheduled for the next first-Tuesday auction. The county notes that "indexes are no substitute for a professional or legal title and lien search" — meaning use this as a starting point, not a final due diligence tool.

Third-party aggregators like RealtyTrac and foreclosure.com also index El Paso County trustee sale notices, but they may lag the county's own records by days or weeks.

### Bidding at the Courthouse

- **Location:** El Paso County Courthouse, 500 E. San Antonio Ave, El Paso, TX 79901
- **Day:** First Tuesday of every month
- **Time:** Between 10:00 AM and 4:00 PM (per Texas Property Code §51.002)
- **Payment:** Cash or cashier's check payable in full at the time of sale — there is no post-sale financing option
- **Who conducts:** Private trustee or substitute trustee appointed by the lender
- **Deed type:** Substitute Trustee's Deed — no warranty on title

**Property inspection:** You generally cannot access the interior of a property before a trustee sale. Investors typically drive by for an exterior assessment, pull the appraisal district record at the El Paso Central Appraisal District (epcad.org) for tax/legal description details, and run a title search to identify any superior liens or encumbrances.

**What you're inheriting:** At a trustee sale, you're bidding on the lender's interest. First-lien foreclosures typically wipe out junior liens (second mortgages, mechanic's liens filed after the first deed of trust). However, **property tax liens** are always senior to private liens in Texas — you inherit any unpaid taxes regardless of when the lien attached.

## Track 2: Sheriff's Tax Foreclosure Sales

### How Tax Foreclosure Works in Texas

When a property owner fails to pay property taxes, the taxing authority (county, city, school district) can obtain a court judgment and order the property sold to satisfy the delinquent tax debt. In El Paso County, the Sheriff's Office conducts these court-ordered sales.

The process:
1. **Tax delinquency** — Property taxes go unpaid; penalties and interest accrue
2. **Suit filed** — The taxing authority files a civil suit in El Paso County court
3. **Judgment** — Court orders the property sold
4. **Sheriff's Sale notice** — Listings posted on the **15th of each month** at epcounty.com/1192/Sheriff-Sales and on the online auction platform
5. **Online auction** — Property sold via **elpaso.texas.sheriffsaleauctions.com** (RealForeclose, a Realauction.com platform)

### Bidding on the Online Platform

Tax sales in El Paso County are conducted entirely online through elpaso.texas.sheriffsaleauctions.com. Before bidding:

- **Register** at the platform and review the Bidder Letter and User Agreement
- **Review listings** — Tax sale property details, minimum bids, and legal descriptions are posted with each listing
- **Understand minimum bids** — Starting bids are typically set at the amount of delinquent taxes plus penalties, interest, attorney fees, and court costs
- **Payment terms** — Refer to the current platform requirements; these are set by the Realauction.com system and confirmed at registration

The Sheriff's Sales page (epcounty.com/1192/Sheriff-Sales) notes that listed sales are for the Sheriff's Office only and do not include sales by El Paso County Constable offices. If you're researching tax-related judgment sales comprehensively, also check Constable office notices.

### Redemption Rights — Critical for Tax Sale Buyers

Unlike mortgage foreclosures, **Texas tax foreclosure sales carry redemption rights:**

| Property Type | Redemption Period |
|--------------|------------------|
| Homestead or agricultural land | **2 years** from sale date |
| Commercial or non-homestead property | **180 days** from sale date |

During the redemption period, the former owner can reclaim the property by paying the winning bid amount plus a penalty of 25% (first year) or 50% (second year) in the case of homestead/agricultural property. This means you cannot take possession of a homestead property immediately after the tax sale — and even after you take possession, you face the risk of redemption for up to two years.

**Deed type:** Sheriff's Deed (tax deed) — no warranty on title. Tax deeds do not guarantee a clean chain of title. A title search or title insurance commitment before bidding is strongly advisable.

**Lien survival:** Most private liens (mortgages, mechanic's liens) are extinguished by a tax foreclosure sale — but not always. IRS federal tax liens, for example, have a 120-day right of redemption under federal law even after a state tax sale. If the property has federal liens, the IRS can step in and redeem within that window.

## El Paso County's Foreclosure Market in 2026

### Market Context

El Paso's housing market is among the most affordable in Texas. The median home sale price was approximately **$251,000 in March 2026**, down 2.0% year-over-year according to Redfin market data. The El Paso Association of Realtors reported that the 2025 full-year median price reached $274,200 (El Paso Times, February 2026). Homes are sitting on the market longer — about 50 days in early 2026, up from 40 days a year prior.

The distressed pipeline reflects this softening. foreclosure.com currently lists approximately **214 active listings** in some stage of foreclosure in El Paso County. RealtyTrac indexes around **116 foreclosure homes** in the broader El Paso area, with bank-owned properties in the $245,000–$280,000 range.

Nationally, foreclosure activity is climbing. ATTOM's Q1 2026 Foreclosure Market Report found foreclosure starts rose **20% year-over-year** and bank repossessions climbed **45% year-over-year** in Q1 2026. El Paso's numbers are consistent with this post-pandemic normalization trend as forbearance programs fully wind down.

### Fort Bliss and the Military Housing Factor

El Paso County is home to **Fort Bliss**, one of the largest US Army installations in the country, with more than 80,000 soldiers, civilians, and family members. The military population creates a housing dynamic found nowhere else in Texas:

- **PCS (Permanent Change of Station) disruptions** — When servicemembers receive orders and must move quickly, some properties enter distress, especially if they were purchased near a market peak and equity has eroded
- **VA loan prevalence** — VA-backed mortgages make up a larger share of El Paso originations than almost any other Texas metro; VA foreclosure processing timelines can differ from conventional loans
- **Rental market pressure** — Fort Bliss rotation cycles create rental demand spikes, which some investors target instead of resale after purchasing distressed properties

Investors evaluating El Paso foreclosure properties near Fort Bliss (northeast El Paso, El Paso 79908, 79934, 79938 zip codes) should factor in both the PCS-driven demand floor and the potential for quick occupant turnover.

### The Binational Border Context

El Paso shares a border with Ciudad Juárez, Mexico (population ~1.4 million), connected by international bridges including the Bridge of the Americas, the Ysleta-Zaragoza International Bridge, and the Stanton Street Bridge. Cross-border economic cycles — maquiladora industry fluctuations, trade policy changes, currency movements — affect employment and housing demand in neighborhoods closest to the international ports of entry. Properties in lower Valley areas (Ysleta, Socorro, San Elizario) may experience higher distress rates during regional border economy downturns.

Compared to [Harris County (Houston)](/foreclosures/harris-county-tx), El Paso operates the same first-Tuesday Texas foreclosure calendar but with a significantly smaller volume of trustee sale filings — reflecting its smaller population and more affordable price points. Investors active in multiple Texas counties will recognize the same legal framework but a different economic profile.

For investors comparing Sun Belt markets, [Maricopa County (Phoenix)](/foreclosures/maricopa-county-az) and [Clark County (Las Vegas)](/foreclosures/clark-county-nv) offer useful benchmarks — both are high-growth Southwest metros with active distressed property pipelines and different legal frameworks.

## Step-by-Step: How to Bid at an El Paso County Foreclosure Auction

### For Trustee (Mortgage) Sales at the Courthouse

1. **Pull the foreclosure list.** Search apps.epcountytx.gov/publicrecords/Foreclosures by the upcoming first Tuesday date. Download the results and begin filtering by property type and zip code.

2. **Run a title search.** Order a title commitment or at minimum a preliminary title search from a licensed Texas title company or attorney. You need to identify: outstanding first-lien balance, any superior liens, property tax balances, HOA arrears, and any IRS/federal liens.

3. **Check the appraisal district record.** Look up the property at epcad.org (El Paso Central Appraisal District) for appraised value, tax history, and legal description. Cross-reference with recent comparable sales in the area.

4. **Drive the property.** Exterior inspection only — no right to enter without the owner's permission before the sale. Note condition, access, any visible damage.

5. **Determine your maximum bid.** Calculate: ARV (after-repair value) minus estimated repair costs minus your required margin minus property tax arrears (which you'll owe even after the sale). The minimum bid at a trustee sale equals the outstanding loan balance plus fees; you rarely need to bid much above that.

6. **Prepare payment.** Bring a cashier's check made payable to yourself, which you endorse to the trustee at time of winning. Bring enough to cover your maximum bid. Know the denominations accepted.

7. **Arrive at the courthouse.** Arrive early at 500 E. San Antonio Ave on the first Tuesday. Auction runs between 10 AM and 4 PM. Each property is called and auctioned individually. No pre-registration required for mortgage foreclosure sales.

8. **Win and pay.** If you win, pay immediately. The trustee issues a receipt and will deliver the Substitute Trustee's Deed within days.

9. **Record the deed and pay taxes.** Record your deed at the El Paso County Clerk's Office. Pay any outstanding property taxes to avoid a lien accruing on your ownership.

### For Tax Foreclosure Sales (Online via RealForeclose)

1. **Monitor listings.** Tax sale listings post on the 15th of each month at elpaso.texas.sheriffsaleauctions.com. Review property information, legal descriptions, and minimum bid amounts.

2. **Register on the platform.** Complete registration at elpaso.texas.sheriffsaleauctions.com. Read the Bidder Letter and User Agreement before submitting your first bid.

3. **Conduct title research.** For tax sales especially, a full title search is critical. Determine whether the property is homestead (2-year redemption) or commercial (180-day redemption). Understand any surviving liens — particularly federal tax liens.

4. **Bid online.** Place your bid through the platform per the current bidding instructions.

5. **Account for redemption.** If the property is a homestead, plan your acquisition assuming the former owner may exercise the 2-year redemption right. Do not invest in major improvements during this window without consulting a Texas real estate attorney.

## Risks and Due Diligence

**No title warranty.** Neither a Substitute Trustee's Deed nor a Sheriff's Tax Deed comes with any warranty on the chain of title. Title insurance companies may be reluctant to issue a policy immediately after a tax sale or trustee sale without seasoning. Build title curative costs into your acquisition analysis.

**Occupied properties.** Properties sold at trustee or tax sales may be occupied — by the former owner, a tenant, or in some cases a squatter. Texas landlord-tenant law governs eviction even after a valid foreclosure sale. Budget time and legal fees for the eviction process if necessary.

**Property tax inheritance.** At a trustee sale, outstanding property taxes are the buyer's responsibility. At a tax sale, the delinquent taxes that triggered the sale are satisfied by the sale — but post-sale taxes are your obligation immediately.

**Federal liens.** IRS federal tax liens survive most state foreclosure processes for a period. Confirm whether the IRS has a recorded lien on the property before bidding.

**Environmental hazards.** West Texas properties, especially industrial or commercial distressed assets, may carry environmental liability. Phase I environmental assessments are standard practice for commercial acquisitions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where do I find the El Paso County foreclosure auction list?**  
For mortgage (trustee) foreclosures, search apps.epcountytx.gov/publicrecords/Foreclosures by the upcoming first Tuesday date. For tax foreclosure sales, listings are posted monthly at elpaso.texas.sheriffsaleauctions.com and at epcounty.com/1192/Sheriff-Sales.

**What payment is accepted at the El Paso County courthouse auction?**  
Cash or cashier's check payable in full at the time of winning. No post-bid financing is allowed. There is no grace period to arrange funds after bidding.

**Is there a right of redemption after buying at a Texas foreclosure auction?**  
For mortgage (trustee) foreclosures: **No.** Texas has no right of redemption for non-judicial foreclosure sales. For tax foreclosure sales: **Yes.** Homestead and agricultural properties have a 2-year redemption period; commercial properties have 180 days.

**Can I inspect a property before bidding at a trustee sale?**  
You have no legal right to interior access before a trustee sale. Exterior viewing is standard. Order property records from the El Paso Central Appraisal District (epcad.org) and run a title search to gather as much information as possible.

**How is El Paso County, Texas different from El Paso County, Colorado?**  
El Paso County, Colorado (Colorado Springs) uses Colorado's Public Trustee system — a completely different legal framework. Colorado foreclosures are processed through the county Public Trustee and available at `elpasoco.com`. Texas El Paso County uses the Texas non-judicial process outlined in this guide.

**What is the El Paso County Foreclosure auction schedule?**  
Trustee (mortgage) foreclosure auctions are held on the first Tuesday of every month at the El Paso County Courthouse. Tax foreclosure sale listings post on the 15th of each month; sale dates vary and are listed at epcounty.com/1192/Sheriff-Sales and the online RealForeclose platform.
