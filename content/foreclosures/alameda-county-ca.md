# Alameda County Foreclosure Auction: A Complete Investor's Guide

Alameda County sits at the heart of the Bay Area real estate market, anchored by Oakland, Berkeley, and Fremont. For real estate investors, the **Alameda County foreclosure auction** landscape is worth understanding — but it's more nuanced than most guides let on. The county operates two entirely separate foreclosure auction systems, each with different rules, venues, risks, and opportunities.

---

## Two Types of Foreclosure Auctions in Alameda County

### 1. Trustee Sales (Non-Judicial Foreclosures)

The vast majority of residential foreclosures in California, including Alameda County, are handled through the **non-judicial trustee sale process**.

**Venue:** Trustee sales in Alameda County are held at the **bottom of the Fallon Street steps at the Rene C. Davidson Courthouse, 1225 Fallon Street, Oakland** (corner of 12th and Oak Streets). Despite the courthouse location, the Alameda County Sheriff's Office does not conduct these sales — private trustee companies run them.

**How to find upcoming sales:** Several aggregator platforms track these notices:
- **auction.com** — largest online marketplace for bank-owned and pre-foreclosure auctions
- **foreclosurelistings.com** — active listings including sheriff sales and pre-foreclosures
- **RealtyTrac** — tracks notices of default and trustee sale filings

### 2. Tax-Defaulted Property Auctions

Separate from mortgage foreclosures, Alameda County periodically auctions **tax-defaulted properties** through **Bid4Assets (bid4assets.com)**.

**To participate in a Bid4Assets tax auction:**
1. Create a free account at bid4assets.com
2. Submit a $5,000 deposit (plus a non-refundable $35 processing fee)
3. A single deposit covers all parcels in that auction cycle

## The California Non-Judicial Foreclosure Timeline

### Step 1: Missed Payments & Pre-Default Contact
Under California's **Homeowner Bill of Rights**, the loan servicer must attempt to contact the borrower before filing a Notice of Default.

### Step 2: Notice of Default (NOD) Recorded
The trustee records a Notice of Default with the county recorder, kicking off a **90-day reinstatement period**.

### Step 3: Notice of Trustee Sale (NTS) Recorded
The trustee records a Notice of Trustee Sale at least **21 days before the auction date**.

### Step 4: The Auction
- **Payment:** Cash or cashier's check only
- **Opening bid:** Set by the lender
- **Winning:** Highest bidder takes the property via **Trustee's Deed Upon Sale**

### Step 5: Post-Sale Rights Window
Under California Civil Code §2924m, owner-occupants and eligible tenants have a **45-day exclusive purchase window** after the trustee sale.

### Step 6: If No Bidder — REO
If no bidder meets or exceeds the lender's opening bid, the property reverts to the lender as REO.

## Investor Due Diligence: What Matters Most in Alameda County

### Title and Lien Research
Trustee sales extinguish junior liens but **do not** wipe out senior liens, IRS liens, or property tax obligations. Always run a preliminary title report before bidding.

### Occupancy Status
Oakland and Berkeley have some of California's strongest **tenant protection ordinances**. Properties in those cities may have tenants legally entitled to extended occupancy, relocation assistance, or both.

### Property Condition
Courthouse-step buyers cannot inspect the interior before the auction. Do drive-bys, review permit histories, and build in a substantial contingency for repairs.

### Competition and Opening Bids
Alameda County is a high-demand market. Opening bids often reflect significant underlying loan balances. Know your ARV (after-repair value) before you bid.
