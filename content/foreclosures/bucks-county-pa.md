# Bucks County PA Foreclosure Auction: Complete Bidder's Guide

If you're looking to buy property at a **Bucks County PA foreclosure auction**, understanding the sheriff sale process is essential before you place your first bid. Bucks County runs a judicial foreclosure system governed by Pennsylvania state law, with monthly sales open to the public at the county seat in Doylestown. This guide covers every step — from the legal notice requirements that trigger foreclosure to what happens after the gavel falls.

---

## How Pennsylvania's Judicial Foreclosure Process Works

Pennsylvania is a **judicial foreclosure state**, meaning every residential mortgage foreclosure must pass through the Court of Common Pleas before a property can be auctioned. There is no non-judicial (trustee-sale) shortcut available to lenders. This court involvement gives homeowners more time to respond, cure the default, or pursue alternatives — but it also means the timeline from first missed payment to sheriff sale can stretch 12 to 18 months or longer.

### PA Act 6 and Act 91 Pre-Foreclosure Notices

Before filing suit, Pennsylvania law requires lenders to send formal pre-foreclosure notices:

- **Act 6 Notice** (41 P.S. §§ 101–605): Applies to most residential mortgage loans. The lender must send a written notice via first-class and certified mail giving the borrower at least **30 days** to cure the default before a complaint is filed. The notice must state the amount due, the lender's intent to accelerate the full loan balance, and the borrower's right to assert defenses in court.

- **Act 91 Notice** (35 P.S. §§ 1680.401c et seq.): Required for owner-occupied residential properties and accompanies the Act 6 notice. It informs the homeowner of their right to apply for assistance through Pennsylvania's **Homeowner Assistance Fund (HAF)** and the Housing Finance Agency. The borrower has 30 days from receipt to apply before foreclosure can proceed.

Failure by the lender to properly serve these notices can be grounds to challenge the foreclosure in court.

### Filing the Foreclosure Complaint

Once pre-foreclosure notice requirements are satisfied and the borrower has not cured the default, the lender (plaintiff) files a **Complaint in Mortgage Foreclosure** with the Bucks County Court of Common Pleas. The complaint must include a title search, the mortgage document, and evidence of the default.

After filing, the defendant (borrower) has **20 days** to file a written response. If no response is filed or the court finds the borrower in default, the plaintiff petitions for a default judgment. Upon entry of judgment, the plaintiff files a **Writ of Execution**, which directs the Bucks County Sheriff to schedule and conduct a public sale of the property.

## The Bucks County Sheriff Sale: What Bidders Need to Know

The **Bucks County PA foreclosure auction** is officially called a Sheriff's Sale and is administered by the Bucks County Sheriff's Office.

### When and Where Sales Are Held

Sheriff's Sales take place on the **second Friday of each month at 11:00 AM** (prevailing time). All sales are conducted in person at:

**Bucks County Administration Building**  
55 E. Court Street  
Doylestown, PA 18901

The Sheriff's Office main location (for registration inquiries) is:

**Bucks County Justice Center**  
100 N Main Street, Floor B2  
Doylestown, PA 18901  
Phone: **215-348-6124**  
Hours: Monday–Friday, 8 AM–4:30 PM

Sales are open to the public. Sale lists are advertised in the **Bucks County Law Reporter** and a local newspaper (The News Herald or The Advance) once per week for three consecutive weeks prior to the sale, and by posting sheriff handbills on the subject property.

### Registration Requirements

In-person registration is **required** if you plan to bid. Registration opens at **10:00 AM** — one hour before the sale begins. To register you must bring:

- Government-issued **photo ID**
- **Proof of funds** (cash or certified check)
- A completed **Real Estate Sale Bid Registration Form** (available from the Sheriff's Office or on the Bucks County website)

Walk-ins are accepted on sale day; there is no pre-registration deadline. However, showing up unprepared with cash or a certified check means you cannot bid.

### What "AS-IS" Really Means

Every property at a Bucks County sheriff sale is sold with **no warranties whatsoever** — no structural, environmental, or title guarantees. The Sheriff is not liable for any condition of the property. Buyers are strongly encouraged to:

- Conduct a **title search** before bidding (check the Recorder of Deeds, Prothonotary, and Tax Claim Bureau)
- Review any **municipal liens** with local tax collectors, water/sewer authorities, and utilities
- Check **zoning** and corporate liens through the Pennsylvania Department of Revenue
- Consult a **real estate attorney** or title company before committing to a bid

### Online Bidding via Bid4Assets

Bucks County has also listed select sheriff sale properties on **Bid4Assets** (bid4assets.com), a national online foreclosure auction platform. When a property appears on Bid4Assets, the auction is conducted online rather than at the courthouse. Check both the Bucks County Sheriff's Sale Listings page and Bid4Assets to ensure you don't miss active auctions.

## Adjournments, Postponements, and Stays

Not every property that appears on the sale list will actually be auctioned on the scheduled date. Properties can be:

- **Postponed**: The sale is moved to a future date. A property may be adjourned up to **130 days** from the original sale date, and up to **two postponements** are permitted without a court order. Subsequent postponements require a court order under Pa. R.C.P. 3129.3.
- **Stayed**: The sale is completely halted (e.g., due to a bankruptcy filing or loan modification agreement).
- **Removed**: Withdrawn from sale by proper authority at any time before the auction.

Arrive on sale day prepared for the list to change — properties are routinely adjourned on short notice.

## Post-Sale Rights and Obligations

### No Statutory Right of Redemption

Pennsylvania **does not grant homeowners a statutory right of redemption** after a sheriff sale. Once the sale is confirmed and the deed is transferred, the former owner cannot reclaim the property by repaying the debt. This is a significant distinction from states like Michigan or Alabama where post-sale redemption periods exist.

### Deficiency Judgments

If the sheriff sale proceeds are less than the total debt owed (mortgage balance + interest + costs), the lender may pursue a **deficiency judgment** against the former borrower for the difference. Pennsylvania law limits the deficiency to the difference between the debt and the **fair market value** of the property at the time of the sale — not necessarily the sale price — providing some protection to former owners.

### Deed Recording and Transfer

After the sale, the Sheriff executes and delivers a **Sheriff's Deed** to the winning bidder. The deed is recorded with the Bucks County **Recorder of Deeds**. The new owner then takes title subject to any surviving liens not extinguished by the sheriff sale (typically, some municipal charges and taxes may survive).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**1. How do I find the list of upcoming Bucks County PA foreclosure auction properties?**  
The Sheriff's Sales Listings are published on the official Bucks County website at buckscounty.gov under the Sheriff's Office section. Lists are also advertised in the Bucks County Law Reporter and local newspapers approximately three weeks before each sale. Some properties appear on Bid4Assets for online bidding.

**2. Can I inspect a property before bidding at the Bucks County sheriff sale?**  
No. The Bucks County Sheriff's Office explicitly prohibits inspection of sale properties. Properties are sold "as-is" and buyers must rely on exterior viewing, public records, and any due diligence they can conduct without entering the property. Consult a title company and review municipal records before bidding.

**3. What happens if I win the bid but can't pay in full on sale day?**  
You must have cash or a certified check ready on auction day. Failure to complete payment can result in forfeiture of any deposit paid and potential legal liability. There is no financing contingency — come prepared with funds in hand.

**4. Are there any redemption rights after buying at a Bucks County PA foreclosure auction?**  
No. Pennsylvania does not provide a post-sale statutory redemption right for the former homeowner. Once confirmed by the court and the deed is transferred, the sale is final. As the buyer, you obtain title at the conclusion of the sale process.

**5. What liens survive a sheriff sale in Bucks County?**  
Not all liens are extinguished at the sheriff sale. Municipal utility charges, certain tax liens, and other priority claims may survive. This is why a thorough title search through the Bucks County Recorder of Deeds, Prothonotary, Tax Claim Bureau, and local tax offices is essential before placing any bid. An attorney specializing in real estate can help identify surviving encumbrances.
