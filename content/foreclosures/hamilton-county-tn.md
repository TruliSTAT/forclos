## How Tennessee Non-Judicial Foreclosure Works

Tennessee is a **non-judicial foreclosure state**, which means lenders don't need court approval to foreclose on a property. Instead, the process is governed by **Tenn. Code Ann. (TCA) Title 35, Chapter 5** — specifically §§ 35-5-101 through 35-5-118 — along with the terms written directly into the deed of trust.

When a Tennessee borrower finances a home purchase, they typically sign a **deed of trust** rather than a traditional mortgage. The deed of trust contains a **power-of-sale clause** that grants the lender (or a designated trustee) the legal authority to sell the property at public auction if the borrower defaults — without filing a lawsuit. This power-of-sale mechanism is the engine that drives Tennessee's non-judicial system and is why Hamilton County foreclosure auctions can move significantly faster than those in judicial states.

### The Two-Track Framework

Tennessee's non-judicial process operates on what courts call a **"two-track" framework**: the foreclosing party must satisfy both:

1. **Statutory requirements** under TCA Title 35, Chapter 5
2. **Contractual requirements** written into the deed of trust itself

A deficiency in either track can create grounds to challenge a completed sale.

## Step-by-Step: The Hamilton County Foreclosure Auction Process

### Step 1 — Missed Payments and Default

Foreclosure begins after a borrower falls behind on mortgage payments. Federal mortgage servicing regulations under the CFPB's Regulation X generally prohibit servicers of federally backed loans from initiating foreclosure until a borrower is more than 120 days delinquent.

### Step 2 — Right-to-Foreclose Notice (60 Days Before First Publication)

Before any public notice is published, the lender must send a **notice of the right to foreclose** to the borrower. Under **TCA § 35-5-117(e)**, this notice must be delivered **at least 60 days before the first publication** of the foreclosure sale notice.

### Step 3 — Public Notice of Sale (20+ Days Before Auction)

Under **TCA § 35-5-101(b)**, the Substitute Trustee must publish the foreclosure sale notice in a **newspaper of general circulation** in the county where the property is located. In Hamilton County, sale notices appear in the **Hamilton County Herald**. Under H.B. 1155, effective July 1, 2025, publication is required **two times**, with the first publication appearing **at least 20 days before the sale date**. All foreclosure sale notices must also be posted online at **foreclosuretennessee.com**.

### Step 4 — The Hamilton County Foreclosure Auction

Hamilton County TN foreclosure auctions are typically conducted at the **west door of the Hamilton County Courthouse, 625 Georgia Avenue, Chattanooga, TN 37402**, usually commencing at **approximately 11:00 AM local time**. Key bidding facts:

- The **opening bid** is typically set by the lender and equals the outstanding balance plus costs. If no third party bids above this amount, the lender acquires title via a **credit bid**.
- Winning bidders must pay with **certified funds** — typically a cashier's check made payable to the Substitute Trustee.
- A **deposit** (commonly 10% of the winning bid) is often required immediately at the auction, with the balance due at closing (typically within 30 days).
- All properties are sold **"as-is, where-is"** with no title warranties.

### Step 5 — Substitute Trustee's Deed and Title Transfer

Once the winning bidder pays in full, the Substitute Trustee executes a **Substitute Trustee's Deed** conveying title to the purchaser. This deed is recorded with the **Hamilton County Register of Deeds** at 625 Georgia Avenue, Chattanooga.

## Post-Sale Rights: Redemption in Tennessee

Tennessee statutes technically provide a **two-year right of redemption** under TCA §§ 66-8-101 through 66-8-103. However, **standard Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac deed of trust forms expressly waive the statutory redemption right**, meaning most residential homeowners foreclosed under a conventional loan have **no post-sale redemption period**. Confirm whether any redemption period survives before bidding.

## Delinquent Tax Sales vs. Trustee Sales: Know the Difference

Hamilton County hosts **two distinct types of property auctions** that buyers frequently confuse:

**1. Trustee's Sale (Non-Judicial Mortgage Foreclosure)**  
Conducted by the lender's Substitute Trustee — typically at the courthouse — involving properties where the mortgage is in default. Title transfers by Substitute Trustee's Deed.

**2. Delinquent Property Tax Sale (Clerk & Master)**  
Administered by the **Hamilton County Clerk & Master** (Phone: (423) 209-6606) and held annually — typically in June — involving properties with delinquent property tax obligations. Bidders acquire a tax lien interest.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Where are Hamilton County TN foreclosure auctions held?**
Most trustee's sales take place at the west door of the Hamilton County Courthouse, 625 Georgia Avenue, Chattanooga, TN 37402, typically starting at approximately 11:00 AM local time. Always confirm the exact location and time in the published notice of sale.

**Q: Do I need to register in advance to bid at a Hamilton County foreclosure auction?**
For trustee's sales (non-judicial mortgage foreclosures), advance registration is generally not required — you may attend and bid on auction day. However, you must bring sufficient certified funds.

**Q: What happens to existing liens after a trustee's sale?**
The foreclosing lender's deed of trust is extinguished, along with most **junior liens** recorded after it. However, **senior liens** — including prior recorded mortgages and unpaid property tax liens — typically survive the foreclosure sale.

**Q: How long does the full foreclosure process take in Hamilton County?**
Tennessee law requires a minimum of approximately **80 days** from the right-to-foreclose notice through the auction date (60-day notice + 20-day publication period). In practice, most Hamilton County TN foreclosure auctions complete within **60–120 days** of the lender initiating formal action.

**Q: Can a homeowner stop the foreclosure sale before auction day?**
Yes — up until the moment the Substitute Trustee announces the sale open, a homeowner may halt the foreclosure by paying the full outstanding balance, negotiating a workout agreement, completing a refinance or home sale, or filing for bankruptcy protection.
