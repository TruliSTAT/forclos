# Travis County Foreclosure Listings: Your Complete Guide to Buying at Auction in Austin, TX

Travis County foreclosure listings draw buyers from across Central Texas and beyond — and for good reason. Austin's real estate market has remained one of the most dynamic in the country, and foreclosure auctions offer a potential path to below-market acquisitions. But buying foreclosures in Travis County means understanding a layered process: Texas law governs the mechanics, the county runs its own auction platform, and the pace is fast.

---

## What Drives Foreclosure Activity in Travis County?

Travis County encompasses Austin, the state capital. Property values here rank among the highest in Texas, which means fewer deeply distressed deals — but properties that do come to auction tend to generate intense bidder competition.

Foreclosure inventory breaks down into two categories:
- **Mortgage/Trustee Foreclosures** — initiated by lenders when homeowners default on loans
- **Tax Foreclosures** — initiated by the county when property taxes go unpaid

---

## Texas Foreclosure Law: How Non-Judicial Foreclosures Work

Texas is a **non-judicial foreclosure state**, governed by **Texas Property Code Section 51.002**.

1. **Borrower defaults** on their mortgage loan
2. **Lender sends notice** of default and opportunity to cure
3. **Trustee files a Notice of Sale** with the Travis County Clerk — at least **21 days** before the auction date
4. **Sale occurs on the first Tuesday of the following month**, between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m.
5. The trustee opens bidding with a **credit bid** on behalf of the lender
6. Third-party bidders must **pay with certified funds on the day of sale**
7. The highest bidder receives a **Trustee's Deed** — no warranties included

**Important exception:** Home equity loans and reverse mortgages require an **Expedited Foreclosure Proceeding** under Texas Rules of Civil Procedure 735–736.

---

## No Redemption Rights in Texas Mortgage Foreclosures

**Texas has no statutory redemption period for mortgage foreclosures.** Once the gavel falls at a trustee sale, the prior owner cannot reclaim the property by paying the debt after the fact.

*(Note: Tax lien foreclosures are different — homestead and agricultural properties have a **2-year right of redemption**.)*

## Travis County's Auction Platform: RealAuction

Travis County has moved its tax foreclosure auctions to an **online platform** — **RealAuction**, accessible at [travis.texas.realforeclose.com](https://travis.texas.realforeclose.com).

Trustee (mortgage) sales remain at the **West steps of the Travis County Courthouse, 1000 Guadalupe St., Austin, TX 78701**.

Key dates: sales are scheduled for the **first Tuesday of each month at 10 a.m.**

## How to Register and Bid at Travis County Tax Sales

1. **Register with RealAuction** at travis.texas.realforeclose.com
2. **Register with the Travis County Tax Office** — submit the Bidder Registration form and pay the **$10 registration fee** at least **5 business days** before the sale date
3. **Wait for eligibility approval** — the Tax Office verifies no outstanding Travis County property tax delinquencies
4. **Registration is valid for one year**

On sale day, winning bidders must pay with **certified funds** immediately. Properties are sold "as is."

---

## Bidding Strategy and Due Diligence

- **Search the title** — additional liens may survive the sale
- **Drive by the property** — interior access is not available before auction
- **Research the TCAD appraisal** at TCAD.org
- **Check for occupants** — eviction adds time and cost
- **Know your ceiling** — set a firm maximum before the auction starts

For homestead and agricultural properties bought at **tax foreclosure**, plan for the 2-year redemption risk.
