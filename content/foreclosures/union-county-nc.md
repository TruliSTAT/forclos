## How NC Foreclosure Auctions Work: 8 Steps From Default to Deed

North Carolina uses a **power-of-sale foreclosure** process governed by **Article 2A of Chapter 45 of the NC General Statutes**. Unlike many states, NC requires the **Clerk of Superior Court** to authorize each foreclosure before the property can be sold.

**Step 1 — Borrower Default**  
The homeowner stops making mortgage payments. After a notice period, the loan servicer instructs the trustee to begin foreclosure proceedings.

**Step 2 — Notice of Hearing Filed**  
The trustee files a **Notice of Hearing** with the Union County Clerk of Superior Court (400 North Main Street, Monroe, NC 28112). The borrower receives at least 10 days' notice.

**Step 3 — Clerk's Hearing**  
The Clerk examines four issues: valid debt, default, proper notice, and power-of-sale clause (NCGS §45-21.16).

**Step 4 — Notice of Sale Published**  
Once authorized, the trustee publishes a Notice of Sale for a minimum of 20 days before the auction.

**Step 5 — Public Auction**  
The union county nc foreclosure auction is held at the **Union County Judicial Center, 400 North Main Street, Monroe, NC**.

**Step 6 — Report of Sale Filed**  
The trustee reports the successful bid to the Clerk of Superior Court. This filing starts the 10-day upset bid period.

**Step 7 — Upset Bid Period (10 Business Days)**  
Any member of the public may file an upset bid within 10 days. Each upset bid restarts the 10-day clock.

**Step 8 — Deed Conveyed**  
Once the upset bid period expires, the trustee conveys a **trustee's deed** to the winning bidder. Title insurance is strongly recommended.

*Source: NC General Statutes Chapter 45, Article 2A (ncleg.gov); NC Courts (nccourts.gov)*

## Bidding at the Union County Foreclosure Auction

### Preparing to Bid

1. **Run a title search** — Know what liens survive the sale
2. **Drive the property** — You typically cannot inspect the interior before auction
3. **Set a firm maximum bid** — Factor in all repair costs and back taxes
4. **Bring funds** — Typically **5–10% of the winning bid** in a cashier's check

### The Upset Bid Process (NCGS §45-21.27)

Under **NCGS §45-21.27**, any person may file an upset bid within 10 days of the report of sale:

- Must exceed the prior bid by at least **5%**, with a **minimum increase of $750**
- Upset bidder must deposit at least **5% of the upset bid** with the Clerk
- Each upset bid restarts a fresh 10-day window

**Example:** You win at $200,000. Someone files an upset bid for $210,500. A new 10-day clock begins.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Where exactly does the Union County NC foreclosure auction take place?**  
A: **Union County Judicial Center, 400 North Main Street, Monroe, NC 28112**. Confirm location with the Clerk at (704) 698-3100.

**Q: How do I find out when the next Union County foreclosure auction is scheduled?**  
A: Contact the Clerk of Superior Court at (704) 698-3100 or search nccourts.gov.

**Q: Can I get a mortgage to buy a foreclosure at a Union County auction?**  
A: Generally no. Properties require cash payment on sale day.

**Q: What happens to existing liens when I buy at auction?**  
A: The foreclosure wipes out junior liens but **IRS tax liens** survive for 120 days, and **property tax liens** may survive. A thorough title search is essential.

**Q: Is the property in good condition?**  
A: There is no guarantee. Properties are sold as-is.
