## Pennsylvania's Judicial Foreclosure Framework

Pennsylvania is a **judicial foreclosure state**, meaning every residential mortgage foreclosure must pass through the court system before a property can be sold at sheriff sale. There is no non-judicial or trustee-sale shortcut. The process is governed primarily by **42 Pa.C.S. §§ 8101–8153** (the judicial sale statutes) and Pennsylvania Rules of Civil Procedure 1141–1179.

### The Pre-Foreclosure Notice Requirements

Before a lender can even file a complaint with the Court of Common Pleas, Pennsylvania law imposes two critical homeowner protections:

**Act 91 Notice (Act 91 of 1983):** When a borrower falls 60 or more days delinquent on a residential mortgage, the lender must send a written Act 91 Notice via first-class and certified mail. This notice informs the homeowner of the delinquency, describes available foreclosure prevention programs, and gives the borrower **30 days** to apply for assistance through the Pennsylvania Homeowner Assistance Fund (PHAF) or similar programs. The lender cannot file a foreclosure complaint until this notice period expires and the application, if filed, is resolved.

**Act 6 Notice (Homeowner's Emergency Mortgage Assistance Program — Act 6 of 1974):** Act 6 provides additional protections for residential mortgages, including the right to cure a default before final judgment. Under Act 6, borrowers may pay all arrears, costs, and reasonable attorney fees at any time **up to one hour before** the sheriff sale to halt the proceeding. This right of cure is a powerful tool that many distressed homeowners overlook.

### From Complaint to Judgment

Once the pre-foreclosure notice period expires, the lender files a **Mortgage Foreclosure Complaint** with the Delaware County Court of Common Pleas. The borrower is personally served and has 20 days to file an Answer. If no Answer is filed, the lender moves for default judgment. If an Answer is filed, the case proceeds through litigation. Once judgment is entered, the lender files a **Praecipe for Writ of Execution**, directing the Delaware County Sheriff to schedule and conduct the sale.

The entire process — from first missed payment to sheriff sale — typically takes **12 to 24 months** in Pennsylvania, longer than many other states due to judicial oversight requirements.

## Delaware County PA Foreclosure Listings: The Sheriff Sale Process

Delaware County sheriff sales are conducted by the **Office of the Sheriff**, currently under Sheriff Siddiq Kamara, based at Courthouse Room 101, 201 W. Front St., Media, PA 19063. The Real Estate Department handles all foreclosure auction logistics and can be reached at **610-891-4305**.

### When and Where Sales Happen

Delaware County holds sheriff sales on the **third Friday of each month at 11:00 AM**. If the third Friday falls on a holiday, the sale moves to the preceding Thursday. Sales are conducted in person in the **County Council Meeting Room on the first floor of the Government Center Building**, Delaware County Court House, Front Street & Veterans Square, Media, PA.

### Finding Delaware County PA Foreclosure Listings

Properties scheduled for sheriff sale are advertised in several ways:
- **Handbills** posted at the Sheriff's Office, Courthouse Room 101
- **Newspaper advertisements** run once a week for three consecutive weeks before the sale
- **Online lists** updated on the official Delaware County website at [delcopa.gov/sheriff](https://www.delcopa.gov/sheriff)
- **Printed list** available for purchase at the sale for **$5.00**

**Important limitation:** Properties scheduled for Delaware County PA foreclosure listings are **not available for interior inspection** before the sale. Bidders purchase at their own risk regarding condition.

## Step-by-Step Guide to Bidding at a Delaware County Sheriff Sale

### Step 1: Research the Property

Before bidding on any Delaware County foreclosure listing, conduct thorough due diligence:
- Check title at the **Delaware County Recorder of Deeds** for existing mortgages, liens, judgments, and encumbrances
- Review the property's tax status with the **Delaware County Tax Claim Bureau** (610-891-8294)
- Search municipal code violations and open permits through the applicable township or borough
- Drive by the exterior to assess visible condition

### Step 2: Attend the Sale Prepared

Arrive at the Government Center Building in Media before 11:00 AM with:
- **Certified check** for the hand money (deposit) — personal checks and cash are not accepted
- Photo identification
- Knowledge of your maximum bid

### Step 3: Bid and Pay Hand Money

The sheriff sale is a **live public auction**. When you win a bid, you must immediately pay **hand money** (the deposit) by certified check. Hand money is typically **10% of the bid price** (verify the current Conditions of Sale document at delcopa.gov for exact requirements).

### Step 4: Pay the Balance

The **balance of your winning bid** is due within **ten calendar days** of the sale, payable to the Sheriff's Office by certified check. Failure to pay the balance results in:
- **Forfeiture of your entire hand money deposit**
- The property being relisted for sale

### Step 5: Deed Transfer

After the balance is paid and the sale is confirmed by the court, the Sheriff issues a **Sheriff's Deed** transferring title to the winning bidder.

## Post-Sale Rights: What Happens After the Auction

### No Statutory Right of Redemption

Unlike some states, **Pennsylvania does not provide a post-sale statutory right of redemption** for residential mortgage foreclosures. Once the sheriff sale is confirmed and the Sheriff's Deed is recorded, the former owner has **no legal right to reclaim the property** by paying off the debt.

### Lien Survival Warning

While a mortgage foreclosure sale generally **wipes out junior liens**, some liens survive the sale, including:
- **Municipal utility liens** for water/sewer
- **Real estate tax claims** that were not properly joined in the foreclosure action
- **Federal tax liens** (IRS has 120 days to redeem after a state foreclosure sale)
- **HOA liens** in some circumstances

Consult a licensed Pennsylvania real estate attorney before bidding.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Delaware County PA Foreclosure Listings

**Q: Where can I find the current Delaware County PA foreclosure listings?**
A: Visit [delcopa.gov/sheriff](https://www.delcopa.gov/sheriff) and navigate to the Real Estate (Sheriff Sale) section. Lists are updated monthly. You can also call the Real Estate Department at 610-891-4305.

**Q: Can I inspect a foreclosure property before the auction?**
A: No. Delaware County sheriff sales are "as-is" — the properties are not open for inspection. Drive-by observation of the exterior is permitted.

**Q: What is hand money, and how much do I need?**
A: Hand money is the deposit you pay immediately when you win a bid. It must be a certified check. The amount is typically 10% of your winning bid.

**Q: What happens if I win but can't pay the balance in 10 days?**
A: You forfeit your entire hand money deposit. The property is re-listed for a future sale. There are no extensions.

**Q: Are Delaware County foreclosure auctions held online?**
A: As of 2026, Delaware County sheriff sales are conducted **in person** at the Government Center Building in Media, PA. They are not held on online platforms like Bid4Assets.
