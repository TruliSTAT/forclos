# Sacramento County Foreclosure Listings: How to Find and Buy Distressed Properties

Sacramento County offers a steady stream of foreclosure opportunities — from pre-foreclosure homes still owned by distressed sellers to bank-owned REOs and properties sold at county tax auctions. But if you're new to the Sacramento distressed property market, you'll quickly find that listings sites show wildly different numbers and the terminology can be confusing. This guide cuts through that noise and explains how foreclosures actually work in Sacramento County, where to find verified listings, and what the buying process looks like at each stage.

---

## How Foreclosure Works in Sacramento County

California is a **non-judicial foreclosure state**, which means mortgage foreclosures happen outside the courtroom — they don't go through a judge or the Sacramento County Sheriff. Instead, the foreclosure process is handled by a trustee (usually a title company) under the power-of-sale clause in the borrower's deed of trust.

This is a key distinction if you've seen the term "sheriff sale" applied to Sacramento County properties. While sheriff sales exist for certain court-ordered judgment liens, the overwhelming majority of residential foreclosures in California proceed through the **trustee sale** process.

### The Non-Judicial Trustee Sale Timeline

1. **Missed Payments** — After a borrower misses roughly three months of mortgage payments, the lender can initiate foreclosure proceedings.
2. **Notice of Default (NOD)** — The lender records a Notice of Default with the Sacramento County Recorder's office. Homeowners have 90 days to catch up on payments.
3. **Notice of Trustee's Sale (NTS)** — At least 21 days before the scheduled auction date, the trustee records and publishes a Notice of Trustee's Sale.
4. **Trustee's Sale (Public Auction)** — The property is sold at a public auction to the highest bidder. Payment typically required in cash or certified funds.
5. **REO (Real Estate Owned)** — If no bidder exceeds the lender's opening bid, the property becomes bank-owned REO.

**Total timeline:** California's non-judicial foreclosure process takes a minimum of approximately 200 days from the first missed payment to trustee sale. There is **no post-sale redemption period** for non-judicial foreclosures in California.

## Sacramento County Tax-Defaulted Property Auctions

Separate from mortgage foreclosures is Sacramento County's **tax-defaulted land auction program**, run by the Sacramento County Tax Collector.

**Key details:**
- **Main annual auction:** Last Monday in February
- **Follow-up auction:** Second Monday in May or June
- **Online auction platform:** [sacramento.mytaxsale.com](https://sacramento.mytaxsale.com/)
- **Registration deposit:** $5,000 required
- **Contact:** Sacramento County Tax-Defaulted Land Unit, (916) 874-6621 or Finance-TDL@saccounty.gov

Important: **Sacramento County sells tax deeds directly**, not tax lien certificates.

## Types of Foreclosure Listings in Sacramento County

### Pre-Foreclosure
The owner has received a Notice of Default but the property has not yet gone to auction. The owner still has the legal right to sell.

### Trustee Sale (Active Auction)
Property has a scheduled auction date. Buyers must have certified funds, accept the property as-is, and assume responsibility for any liens not extinguished by the sale.

### REO (Bank-Owned)
The bank took title at auction. REOs are listed through standard MLS channels and allow inspections.

### Tax-Defaulted Properties
Sold by the county for unpaid property taxes. Deeded sales — no mortgage liens survive.

## What to Know Before You Bid

**At a trustee sale:**
- Bring certified funds (cashier's check)
- You're buying the property as-is, with no inspection contingency
- Research for unpaid HOA dues, IRS liens, and senior liens
- Open bids are set by the lender

**At the Sacramento County tax auction:**
- Register in advance at sacramento.mytaxsale.com
- $5,000 deposit required; non-payment results in deposit forfeiture and a 5-year bidding ban
- Properties are sold as tax deeds

## Protections for Sacramento County Homeowners

California's **Homeowner Bill of Rights** provides:
- Your lender must contact you before starting the foreclosure process
- No dual tracking (pursuing modification while simultaneously foreclosing)
- Options like loan modification, forbearance, repayment plans, and short sale must be explored
