## How King County Foreclosures Work

King County operates under two distinct foreclosure processes, each with its own timeline, participants, and rules.

**Nonjudicial Trustee Sales** apply when a lender forecloses on a property with a deed of trust — the standard mortgage instrument used in Washington State. Under the Washington Deed of Trust Act (RCW 61.24), the lender's trustee can sell the property without going through court, making the process faster than judicial states. Once the lender issues a Notice of Default (NOD), the trustee sale can occur in approximately 120 days.

**King County Treasurer Tax Foreclosure Auction** is a separate government-run process for properties with delinquent property taxes. The county holds an annual public auction through the RealAuction platform at king.wa.realforeclose.com. Properties that fail to sell at auction may be held by the county.

Understanding which type of foreclosure you're dealing with determines where you need to look and what rules apply.

## The Two Tracks: Trustee Sales vs. Tax Foreclosure Auction

### Track 1: Nonjudicial Trustee Sales

These make up the majority of foreclosure activity in King County. The process is:

1. **Notice of Default** — Filed with the county recorder after the borrower is 30+ days delinquent and the lender has given a 30-day cure notice
2. **120-Day Waiting Period** — Washington law (RCW 61.24.030) requires a minimum 120 days between NOD and the trustee sale date
3. **Foreclosure Fairness Act** — For owner-occupied properties, WA's Foreclosure Fairness Act (RCW 61.24.163) entitles borrowers to request mediation through the WA Attorney General's Office — this can pause the timeline
4. **Trustee Sale** — Held at the King County Courthouse or as designated; bidding opens at the lender's opening bid (typically the outstanding loan balance plus fees)
5. **Deed Transfer** — Winning bidder receives a Trustee's Deed; no warranties

**Key Trustee Sale requirement:** Cash-equivalent (cashier's check) for the full bid amount on the day of sale. No financing contingencies.

### Track 2: King County Tax Foreclosure Auction

For properties with multiple years of delinquent property taxes, the King County Treasurer can foreclose and sell the property at a public auction.

- **Platform:** king.wa.realforeclose.com (RealAuction)
- **Next scheduled auction:** September 9, 2026 (confirmed via the official RealAuction platform)
- **How to participate:** Register at the RealAuction platform prior to the auction; deposits typically required
- **Opening bids:** Start at the delinquent tax amount plus fees — often significantly below market value
- **Deed type:** Treasurer's Deed; clears most liens but buyers should conduct thorough title research

## The Annual Tax Foreclosure Auction — How to Participate

The King County Treasurer's annual tax foreclosure auction is held through the RealAuction online platform and historically takes place in late summer or early fall.

**Step 1: Research properties**  
The county publishes a list of properties eligible for auction in advance. Review each property at the King County Assessor's site (blue.kingcounty.com) for tax history, zoning, and parcel data.

**Step 2: Register**  
Create an account at king.wa.realforeclose.com. Registration deadlines occur well before auction day.

**Step 3: Due diligence**  
Tax foreclosure properties are sold "as-is." Arrange a property inspection before bidding. Check for IRS tax liens (federal liens may survive the sale), code violations, and environmental issues.

**Step 4: Fund your account**  
Deposit funds into your RealAuction account prior to the auction. Deposits are required to bid.

**Step 5: Bid**  
Bidding is competitive and online. Opening bids start at the delinquent tax amount. Properties in desirable King County neighborhoods often draw competitive bidding.

**Step 6: Complete the purchase**  
Winning bidders must complete payment promptly. The county issues a Treasurer's Deed.

## Bidding at King County Auctions — What You Need to Know

Whether you're bidding at a trustee sale or the tax auction, preparation is everything:

**Financial requirements:**
- Most trustee sales require full cash payment at the sale — no financing period
- Tax auctions via RealAuction require pre-funded accounts
- Have cashier's checks or verified wire transfer capacity ready

**Title research:**
- Trustee sales wipe out junior liens but may not clear all encumbrances — get a title search first
- Tax sales clear most liens but IRS federal liens (if under 120 days old at time of sale) may survive
- Order a preliminary title report from a title company before bidding

**Property access:**
- Occupied properties cannot be entered prior to sale — exterior inspection only
- Drive-by the property and research from county records

**Common property types at King County auctions:**
- Vacant lots (especially in outer-ring neighborhoods)
- Older SFR with deferred maintenance
- Multi-family units
- Commercial-zoned parcels

## Redemption Rights in Washington State

Washington State's Deed of Trust Act does **not** provide a post-sale redemption period for nonjudicial trustee sales. Once the trustee sale is completed and the deed recorded, the prior owner has no right to reclaim the property.

For tax foreclosure sales, Washington similarly does not provide a post-sale redemption right once the Treasurer's Deed is issued.

However, the **Foreclosure Fairness Act** mediation program gives owner-occupants the opportunity to engage in mediation *before* a trustee sale occurs, potentially modifying their loan or delaying the sale.

This makes the Washington market — especially at trustee sales — a clean-title environment for buyers compared to states with 6–12 month redemption periods.

## Common Mistakes King County Buyers Make

**Relying only on Zillow.** The 7 homes Zillow shows is a fraction of the active market. Use foreclosure.com, foreclosurelistings.com, and the official county auction site to see the full picture.

**Skipping title research.** Even "clean" trustee sales can carry IRS tax liens that survive the foreclosure. Always get a preliminary title report.

**Underestimating renovation costs.** King County's high construction costs ($200–$450/sqft for residential work in 2026) mean deferred-maintenance properties carry substantial rehab budgets.

**Not accounting for occupancy.** If the property is still occupied at sale, you'll need to navigate a lawful eviction process. In Seattle, tenant protections may extend timelines.

**Missing auction registration deadlines.** The RealAuction platform requires pre-registration. Late registrations are not accepted.

**Ignoring environmental issues.** Older properties, especially on hillsides, may have soil contamination or drainage issues. King County's terrain creates additional geotechnical risks.

## FAQ

**Where can I find King County foreclosure listings?**  
The most comprehensive sources are foreclosurelistings.com (4,200+ entries), foreclosure.com (599 active), and the official county tax auction platform at king.wa.realforeclose.com. For bank-owned properties listed on the MLS, check Redfin and foreclosure.com REO section.

**When is the next King County tax foreclosure auction?**  
The next confirmed auction is scheduled for September 9, 2026, through the king.wa.realforeclose.com RealAuction platform. Registration and property lists are published in advance — check the site for current details.

**Can I get financing for a King County foreclosure?**  
For properties purchased at trustee sales or government auctions, conventional financing is typically not available — these require cash or hard money loans. Bank-owned (REO) properties listed on the MLS can often be purchased with conventional financing after closing.

**Does Washington State have a redemption period?**  
No. Washington does not provide a post-sale redemption right for nonjudicial trustee sales or tax foreclosure sales. Once the deed is recorded, the sale is final.

**Do I need to be present at the auction?**  
The King County tax auction is conducted online through RealAuction — you do not need to be physically present. Trustee sales may be held in person or online depending on the trustee.

**What liens survive a trustee sale?**  
Senior liens (first mortgages, IRS tax liens less than 120 days old, property taxes) may survive a trustee sale. Junior liens (second mortgages, mechanic's liens) are typically wiped out. Always get a title report before bidding.
