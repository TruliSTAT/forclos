# Denver County Foreclosure Listings: The Investor's & Homeowner's Complete Guide

Denver County foreclosure listings attract attention for good reason. In a metro where median home values have climbed sharply over the past decade, distressed properties can represent rare entry points at discounts — if buyers understand exactly how the process works.

---

## Current Denver County Foreclosure Listings

Active Denver County foreclosure listings are publicly searchable through the **Denver Public Trustee's official portal** at [denvergov.org/foreclosuresearch](https://denvergov.org/foreclosuresearch/). The portal runs on the new GTS (Government Technology Services) platform, replacing the previous system.

For judicial foreclosures (court-ordered sales), the **Denver Sheriff Department Civil Unit** publishes pending sale lists with case numbers, plaintiff, property address, and scheduled sale dates.

---

## How Denver County Foreclosures Work: Colorado's Non-Judicial System

Colorado is a **non-judicial foreclosure state**. When a borrower defaults on a mortgage secured by a deed of trust, the lender initiates foreclosure through the **Denver Public Trustee** — a government office within the Denver Clerk and Recorder's division.

**Two types of foreclosure auctions happen in Denver County:**

1. **Public Trustee Auctions (Non-Judicial):** Administered by the Denver Public Trustee, these auctions are conducted online through [denver.realforeclose.com](https://www.denver.realforeclose.com/).

2. **Sheriff Auctions (Judicial):** Conducted in-person at **10:00 a.m. on the front steps of the City and County Building, 1437 Bannock St., Denver, CO 80202**.

## Step-by-Step: Denver County Foreclosure Timeline

**Step 1 — Notice of Election and Demand (NED) Filed**
The lender files an NED with the Denver Public Trustee. The Public Trustee records it within **10 business days**.

**Step 2 — Publication Period (~110–125 days)**
A minimum publication period after the NED is recorded before a sale can be held.

**Step 3 — Borrower's Opportunity to Cure**
The homeowner can file an **Intent to Cure** with the Denver Public Trustee at least **15 days before the scheduled auction** to bring the loan current.

**Step 4 — Auction Day**
If the borrower doesn't cure, the property goes to auction. Registered third-party bidders compete. The highest bidder wins a **Certificate of Purchase**.

**Step 5 — Redemption Period**
After the auction, junior lienholders have a statutory period to redeem the property.

**Step 6 — Deed Issuance**
Once all redemption periods expire, the Public Trustee issues a **Confirmation Deed** or **Public Trustee's Deed**.

## How to Find and Bid on Denver Foreclosure Auctions

### For Public Trustee Auctions
1. **Search active listings** at denvergov.org/foreclosuresearch/
2. **Research the property** — pull title history, check for outstanding liens
3. **Drive by the property**
4. **Register and fund your account** at [denver.realforeclose.com](https://www.denver.realforeclose.com/)
5. **Bid on auction day**

### For Sheriff Auctions
1. **Review the pending sale list** on the Denver Sheriff Department website
2. **Bring cash or certified funds** equal to the plaintiff's initial bid plus one dollar
3. **Arrive at 1437 Bannock St. by 10 a.m.**
4. **Submit remaining funds** to the Denver Sheriff Department Civil Unit by **2:00 p.m. the same day**

**Important for both:** Properties at Denver County foreclosure auctions are sold **as-is**. Title complications, hidden liens, and deferred maintenance are the buyer's problem.

## Homeowner Rights: How to Stop a Denver Foreclosure

**Contact the Denver Public Trustee first:**
- **Address:** 200 W. 14th Ave., Denver, CO 80204
- **Phone:** 720-865-2633
- **Email:** foreclosures@denvergov.org
- **Hours:** Monday–Friday, 8:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m.

**Your primary options:**

**1. Cure the Default**
File an Intent to Cure form at least 15 days before the scheduled auction. Payment must be in wire transfer, certified check, cashier's check, or teller's check.

**2. Negotiate a Loan Modification or Forbearance**
Colorado law (C.R.S. § 38-38-103.1) requires lenders to provide a single point of contact for borrowers in foreclosure.

**3. Sell the Property**
If you have equity, a sale before the auction date can pay off the mortgage.

**4. File for Bankruptcy**
A bankruptcy petition triggers an automatic stay that temporarily halts foreclosure proceedings.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does a Denver County foreclosure take?**
From NED filing to auction, approximately 110–125 days minimum. Total time including redemption typically runs 5–8 months.

**Do I need cash to bid at Denver foreclosure auctions?**
For Sheriff auctions: yes. For Public Trustee auctions via realforeclose.com, deposit funds must be on account before you can bid.

**Can a homeowner stop foreclosure after the auction?**
Once the auction occurs and a Certificate of Purchase is issued, the homeowner cannot cure. The redemption period is primarily for junior lienholders.

**What liens survive a Public Trustee foreclosure sale?**
Generally, liens junior to the foreclosing deed of trust are extinguished. Senior liens typically survive.
