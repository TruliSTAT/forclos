## Prince George's County Foreclosure Market Overview

Prince George's County sits at the heart of the DC metro's most underrated investment corridor. Bordered by DC to the west, Montgomery County to the north, and Anne Arundel County to the south, the county combines Metro accessibility — Green, Orange, and Blue lines all run through it — with entry prices that remain significantly below neighboring Montgomery County. The economic base spans federal contractors, University of Maryland, and the ongoing development activity around the National Harbor and the broader Amazon HQ2 spillover zone.

Foreclosure activity in Prince George's County reflects both economic cycles and the county's large population of homeowners with FHA and VA loans. While the post-pandemic foreclosure moratorium held activity low through 2021–2022, filings have normalized over recent years. Prince George's County sees hundreds of foreclosure filings annually, spread across both the mortgage foreclosure track and the county's annual tax lien certificate sale. Investors willing to understand both systems find deal flow year-round.

## How the Maryland Foreclosure Auction Process Works

Maryland is often described as a non-judicial foreclosure state, but the reality is more nuanced — and understanding this distinction is critical before you bid at any prince georges county foreclosure auction.

### Maryland's Power-of-Sale Process: It Goes Through Court

Most residential mortgages in Maryland are structured as deeds of trust. When a borrower defaults, the lender does not simply hold a trustee sale on the courthouse steps the way California or Texas does. Instead, the lender or substitute trustee files an **Order to Docket (OTD)** with the Circuit Court. The court supervises the process and must ratify the sale afterward. This makes Maryland effectively a judicial state in practice, even though the statute is classified differently.

**The standard Maryland mortgage foreclosure timeline:**

1. **Notice of Intent to Foreclose (NOI)** — Sent to the homeowner at least 45 days before court filing; also submitted to the Maryland Office of Financial Regulation within five business days.
2. **Order to Docket filed** — Can be filed as early as 90 days after the first missed payment (120 days for federally-backed loans).
3. **Loss Mitigation Affidavit served** — Homeowner receives notice and has 25 days to request mediation, plus a $50 fee.
4. **Mediation or waiver** — If mediation fails or is waived, the sale is scheduled.
5. **Sale conducted** — Typically by a substitute trustee; notice published in local legal publications.
6. **Court ratification** — The Circuit Court reviews and ratifies the sale.
7. **Deed recorded** — Buyer takes title.

**Important:** Maryland does **not** provide a post-sale redemption period for mortgage foreclosures. Once the court ratifies the sale and the deed records, the prior owner has no statutory right to reclaim the property. This is a meaningful advantage for foreclosure buyers compared to states that allow months of post-sale redemption.

## Finding Prince George's County Foreclosure Listings

No single source captures all active foreclosure inventory in Prince George's County. Here's where to look across both tracks:

### Mortgage Foreclosure Listings
- **Circuit Court case filings** — Orders to Docket filed in the Circuit Court at Upper Marlboro are public record. The court's online docket lets you search active foreclosure cases.
- **MD Land Records (mdlandrec.net)** — Once a deed is recorded post-sale, it appears here. Useful for tracking who bought what and researching titles.
- **SDAT (Maryland State Department of Assessments and Taxation)** — Property ownership and assessment data; cross-reference with court filings to find recently transferred REO properties.
- **Auction.com and RealtyTrac** — National aggregators that pull trustee sale notices. Coverage is uneven, but they catch a substantial share of scheduled sales.
- **Harvey West Auctioneers (hwestauctions.com)** — Check their calendar specifically for Prince George's County court-ordered sales.
- **Legal classifieds** — Sale notices are published in local legal publications; monitoring these can surface properties before they hit the aggregators.

### REO (Bank-Owned) Properties
When a trustee sale fails to attract a third-party bidder, the lender takes title and the property becomes REO. REO inventory typically flows to the MLS within 30–90 days and appears on standard listing platforms alongside other homes.

## Buying at a Prince George's County Foreclosure Auction

Whether you're bidding at a trustee sale in Upper Marlboro or participating in the county's online tax lien auction, the preparation requirements differ — but both demand homework.

### Trustee Sales: What to Expect
- **Cash or certified funds required** — Trustee sales do not allow financing contingencies. Bring a cashier's check for your expected bid; exact requirements vary by trustee.
- **Title is NOT guaranteed** — You acquire whatever interest the foreclosing party held. A title search before bidding is strongly advised; junior liens do not automatically extinguish without a proper chain of notice.
- **No inspection access** — Properties are sold as-is. Drive-bys and exterior assessments are typically all you can do before the auction.
- **Overbidding happens** — Competition in the DC suburbs can push prices close to retail. Build your max bid around a realistic ARV minus rehab and carrying costs.
- **Post-sale registration required** — Prince George's County requires the purchaser to file an Initial Registration at [foreclosure.princegeorgescountymd.gov](http://foreclosure.princegeorgescountymd.gov/) within 30 days of the foreclosure sale ($50 on time / $100 late), and a Final Registration within 30 days of deed recordation.

## Tax Sales in Prince George's County

The county's annual **tax lien certificate sale** is a completely separate channel from mortgage foreclosures — and it comes with mechanics that catch first-time bidders off guard.

### How the Prince George's County Tax Sale Works

The annual tax sale is held on the second Monday in May (the 2025 sale was held May 12). The auction runs entirely online at **taxsale.princegeorgescountymd.gov** — there's no courthouse steps component. Properties are delinquent on county taxes and sold as tax lien certificates, not deeds.

**Registration requirements:**
- Registration window typically opens in mid-April and closes approximately two weeks later
- $150 non-refundable registration fee + $1,000 ACH deposit
- W-9 required

**Bidding format:** Bidders can submit bids as a dollar amount or as a bid factor (a percentage of assessed value). The auction runs in batches, with different property groups closing at 10am, 12pm, 2pm, and 3pm EST on auction day.

### The High Bid Premium: The Trap That Burns First-Timers

This is the most important mechanic to understand before you register.

If your winning bid exceeds **40% of the property's assessed value**, you owe an additional **20% of the excess** as a High Bid Premium. The premium is refundable if the property owner redeems the lien — or if you successfully foreclose on the right of redemption. But if you fail to foreclose within **two years** of the sale date, the premium is forfeited entirely and your certificate becomes void.

**Example:** You win a certificate for a property assessed at $200,000. Your bid is $130,000. The 40% threshold is $80,000. The excess is $50,000. Your High Bid Premium is $10,000 (20% of $50,000). If the owner redeems, you get that $10,000 back. If you don't foreclose within two years, you lose it.

### The Redemption Window and the Two-Year Cliff

After the tax sale, the property owner has a right to redeem the lien by paying the outstanding taxes, interest, and associated costs:
- **9 months** for principal residences
- **6 months** for non-principal residences (rental properties, vacant land)

Once the redemption window closes without payment, the certificate holder may file a complaint in Circuit Court to foreclose on the right of redemption. This is a court process — not a quick administrative step.

Critical deadline: If no foreclosure action is filed within **two years** of the sale date, the certificate is void. The High Bid Premium is not refunded. Plan your timeline accordingly.

Tax sale results for prior years are published on the portal, which is a useful research tool for understanding competitive bid levels before you register.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I find upcoming Prince George's County foreclosure auctions?**  
Check the Circuit Court docket in Upper Marlboro for Orders to Docket, monitor Harvey West Auctioneers for trustee sale calendars, and watch legal classifieds for published sale notices. Auction.com and RealtyTrac aggregate some but not all sales.

**When is the Prince George's County tax sale held?**  
The annual tax lien certificate sale is held on the second Monday in May each year, conducted online at taxsale.princegeorgescountymd.gov. Registration typically opens in April.

**Does Maryland have a post-sale redemption period for mortgage foreclosures?**  
No. Maryland does not provide statutory post-sale redemption rights for mortgage foreclosures. Once the Circuit Court ratifies the sale and the deed is recorded, the prior owner cannot reclaim the property through redemption.

**What is the High Bid Premium at the Prince George's County tax sale?**  
If your winning bid exceeds 40% of the property's assessed value, you owe an additional 20% of the excess as a High Bid Premium. It's refundable upon redemption or successful foreclosure, but forfeited if you fail to file a foreclosure action within two years of the sale.

**Do I need cash to buy at a trustee sale?**  
Yes. Trustee sales in Prince George's County require cash or certified funds. Financing contingencies are not accepted. Confirm the specific deposit requirements with the trustee or auctioneer before the sale.
