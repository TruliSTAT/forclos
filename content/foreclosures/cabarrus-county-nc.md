## How NC Foreclosure Auctions Work

North Carolina uses the **power-of-sale foreclosure** process, governed by **Article 2A of Chapter 45 of the North Carolina General Statutes (NCGS §45-21.1 et seq.)**. Unlike some states, NC requires a **Clerk of Superior Court hearing** before any property can be sold at auction — providing an important legal check in the process. Here are the eight steps every buyer should understand:

**Step 1 — Default**  
The process begins when a borrower fails to make payments or otherwise defaults under their promissory note or deed of trust. This default triggers the lender's right to foreclose.

**Step 2 — 45-Day Pre-Foreclosure Notice**  
Before filing, lenders must send a pre-foreclosure notice to the borrower at least **45 days in advance**, informing them of options such as loan modification or forbearance. The NC Housing Finance Agency administers this requirement through the State Home Foreclosure Prevention Project.

**Step 3 — Petition Filed with the Clerk**  
The trustee (typically a foreclosure attorney) files a petition for foreclosure with the **Clerk of Superior Court** in the county where the property is located — for Cabarrus County properties, that's the Clerk at the Cabarrus County Courthouse, 61 Union St S, Concord, NC 28025.

**Step 4 — Clerk Hearing (NCGS §45-21.16)**  
The Clerk holds a hearing and must find four things before authorizing the sale: (1) a valid debt of which the petitioner is the holder; (2) a default; (3) the right to foreclose under the deed of trust; and (4) proper service of notices. Borrowers may appear and contest these findings. If the Clerk authorizes the sale, the process moves forward.

**Step 5 — Publication and Notice**  
After authorization, the sale must be publicly advertised. Notice is published in a local newspaper **once per week for two consecutive weeks** and posted at the courthouse.

**Step 6 — The Auction Sale**  
The trustee conducts the sale at the announced time and location — typically on the **front steps of the Cabarrus County Courthouse** at 61 Union St S, Concord, NC. The highest cash bidder wins. A cash deposit (usually **5% of the bid price**, paid by certified or cashier's check) is typically required on the spot.

**Step 7 — 10-Day Upset Bid Period (NCGS §45-21.27)**  
After the auction, the trustee files a report of sale with the Clerk. From that filing date, **any person** has **10 days to file an upset bid** — a competing offer that must exceed the reported sale price or the last upset bid by **at least 5% (minimum $750 increase)**. Each new upset bid resets the 10-day window.

**Step 8 — Deed Issued and Recorded**  
Once the upset bid period closes, the trustee executes the deed and the buyer pays the remaining purchase balance. The deed is then recorded at the **Cabarrus County Register of Deeds**.

> **Important:** All sales are subject to cancellation. Properties are sold **AS-IS**, with no warranty of condition.

## Bidding at the Cabarrus County Foreclosure Sale

### Before the Sale
1. Identify upcoming auctions via the official Sheriff's portal
2. Research the property: Run a title search at the Cabarrus County Register of Deeds
3. Drive by the property
4. Set your maximum bid
5. Prepare your deposit: Bring a **certified check or cashier's check** for at least 5% of your intended bid

### The Upset Bid Period
After the sale, the trustee files a report with the Clerk:
- Anyone can submit an upset bid within **10 calendar days**
- The upset bid must exceed the prior bid by at least **5% or $750**
- Each new upset bid resets the 10-day window

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need to register in advance to bid at a Cabarrus County foreclosure auction?**  
A: No pre-registration is required. Simply appear at the **Cabarrus County Courthouse (61 Union St S, Concord, NC)** on the scheduled auction date with your certified check.

**Q: Can I inspect the property before bidding?**  
A: In most cases, no interior inspection is available. Properties are sold AS-IS.

**Q: What happens if I win the auction but someone files an upset bid?**  
A: If an upset bid is filed within 10 days under **NCGS §45-21.27**, your bid is superseded. Your deposit is returned.

**Q: Are there liens on foreclosure properties I should know about?**  
A: Yes. Power-of-sale foreclosure does **not** extinguish property tax liens, IRS tax liens (120-day redemption right), or certain special assessment liens.

**Q: Is Cabarrus County a good market for foreclosure investing in 2025-2026?**  
A: Cabarrus County sits within the **Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia MSA**. Median home prices range from ~$295,000 in Kannapolis to $506,000 in Harrisburg.
