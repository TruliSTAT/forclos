# Pima County Foreclosure Listings: How to Find and Buy Trustee Sale Properties in Tucson, AZ

Pima County foreclosure listings represent one of the more accessible entry points into the Arizona investment property market. With a median home price hovering around $340,000 and a steady pipeline of trustee sales moving through the county, Tucson and its surrounding communities offer real opportunities — if you know where to look and how the Arizona process actually works.

---

## How Arizona Foreclosure Works: Trustee Sales, Not Sheriff Sales

**Arizona is a non-judicial foreclosure state**. When a borrower defaults on a deed of trust in Arizona, the lender does not need to take the borrower to court to foreclose. Instead, the deed of trust appoints a trustee who has the legal authority to sell the property at public auction under Arizona Revised Statutes §33-807.

### The 90-Day Notice Window

Under ARS §33-808, before a trustee can sell a property, they must:
1. **Record a Notice of Trustee's Sale (NOTS)** with the county recorder
2. Mail the NOTS to the borrower, the property owner, and all lienholders
3. **Publish the notice** in a local newspaper once a week for four consecutive weeks
4. **Post the notice** conspicuously on the property itself

All steps must be completed at least **90 days before the scheduled sale date**.

### No Redemption After the Sale

Once a trustee's deed is delivered to the winning bidder, the former owner has **no statutory right of redemption**. The sale is final.

## Where to Find Pima County Foreclosure Listings

### 1. Pima County Recorder's Office

**recorder.pima.gov/PublicSearch**

Every NOTS filed in Pima County is a matter of public record. Search by document type ("Notice of Trustee's Sale" or "NOTS") and date range.

### 2. NoticeRegistry.com

NoticeRegistry aggregates Arizona trustee sale notices, including active Tucson and Pima County sales, in a searchable format.

### 3. Default Research

**defaultresearch.com/states/arizona/counties/pima**

Maintains an upcoming auction calendar for Pima County with venue, time, and trustee information.

### 4. Trustee Corps

**trusteecorps.com**

One of the largest trustee companies in Arizona; publishes an upcoming sale calendar.

### 5. Commercial Aggregators

Sites like Foreclosure.com and RealtyTrac aggregate pre-foreclosure data, bank-owned (REO) listings, and auction properties.

## The Pima County Auction: What to Expect

### Location

Most Pima County trustee sales take place at or near **Pima County Superior Court, 110 W Congress Street, Tucson, AZ 85701**. Always verify the location with the specific trustee before showing up.

### Deposit and Payment Terms

The deposit requirement is typically **$10,000 or 10% of your bid amount, whichever is greater**, due immediately upon winning. The full balance must be paid in certified funds by **5:00 PM mountain time on the next business day** (per ARS §33-811).

### How Bidding Works

Trustee sales use competitive outcry bidding. The lender opens bidding with a credit bid. Third-party investors can then bid higher. All properties are sold **AS-IS**.

## Due Diligence Before Bidding

**Title search:** Trustee sales wipe out junior liens but do not eliminate senior liens (taxes, IRS, HOA liens in some cases). Run a full title search before bidding.

**Property access:** You typically cannot inspect the interior before the auction.

**The 70% Rule:** Many investors use the 70% rule as a ceiling: don't bid more than 70% of the ARV, minus estimated repair costs.

**Occupancy:** If a former owner or tenant is still in the property, you'll need to initiate eviction proceedings.

1. **Set up a recorder search** at recorder.pima.gov/PublicSearch weekly
2. **Cross-reference on NoticeRegistry** for additional property detail
3. **Identify the trustee** from the NOTS
4. **Run your title search** through a Pima County title company
5. **Set your max bid and get your funds** (certified funds, minimum $10,000 or 10%)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Arizona have a redemption period after a trustee sale?**
No. Arizona law provides no redemption right after a non-judicial trustee sale.

**Can I get a home inspection before bidding?**
In most cases, no. Foreclosure properties are sold as-is.

**What happens if no one bids at a Pima County trustee sale?**
If no third-party bids exceed the lender's credit bid, the lender takes ownership (REO).

**Are there taxes or other liens I inherit?**
Potentially. Senior liens — including federal tax liens, HOA super-priority liens — survive the trustee sale.
