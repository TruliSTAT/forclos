# Palm Beach County FL Foreclosure Auction: Listings, Schedule & Bidder's Guide

Florida's sun-soaked Palm Beach County is one of the most dynamic real estate markets in the Southeast — and the **Palm Beach County foreclosure auction** is where investors and homebuyers find properties at discounts that the open market rarely offers. Whether you're targeting a Boynton Beach bungalow, a Delray Beach condo, or a property near Palm Beach Gardens, understanding how the county's auction system works is the first step to placing a winning bid.

---

## How Florida's Judicial Foreclosure Process Works

Florida is a **judicial foreclosure state**, meaning every foreclosure must go through the court system, governed primarily by **Florida Statute §45.031**.

1. **Lis pendens filed** — Lender files notice of lawsuit in Palm Beach County Circuit Court
2. **Lawsuit and default** — The lender serves the borrower; most proceed through uncontested default judgments
3. **Final Judgment of Foreclosure** — A circuit court judge signs the Final Judgment ordering the Clerk of Court to schedule a sale
4. **Sale scheduled** — Under §45.031, the sale must be scheduled no sooner than 20 days and no later than 35 days after the judgment
5. **Online auction** — The Clerk of the Circuit Court & Comptroller conducts the sale online
6. **Certificate of Sale issued** — After the auction, the Clerk issues a Certificate of Sale
7. **10-day objection period** — Any party with standing may object
8. **Certificate of Title issued** — If no valid objection, the Clerk issues the Certificate of Title

**Key point:** Florida eliminated the post-sale right of redemption for mortgage foreclosures. Once the Certificate of Title is issued, the prior owner has no legal right to reclaim the property.

## The Palm Beach County Auction Schedule

- **Platform:** All auctions conducted online through **mypalmbeachclerk.com**
- **Schedule:** Sales run generally **Monday through Thursday, starting at 10:00 a.m. Eastern**
- **Proxy bidding:** Once a case appears on the calendar, proxy bidding opens immediately
- **Contact:** Foreclosures division — (561) 355-2986

Always check the official auction calendar before assuming a property will sell — sales are routinely postponed or cancelled.

## Finding Palm Beach County Foreclosure Listings

### 1. mypalmbeachclerk.com — The Primary Source
The Clerk's official auction page lists all scheduled judicial sales with case number, property address, plaintiff, and auction date.

### 2. pbchomeauctions.com
Aggregates Palm Beach County foreclosure auction data including completed sale prices.

### 3. Court Records Search
Lis pendens filings appear in the Clerk's Official Records as soon as the lawsuit is filed — often 12 to 24 months before a property reaches auction.

## The Bidding Process Step by Step

### Step 1: Register and Set Up a Deposit
Register with the Clerk's designated auction platform. A pre-auction deposit is required. The system supports **auto-bids (proxy bids)**.

### Step 2: Research the Property
- **Order a title search** — Junior mortgage liens and judgment liens may or may not be extinguished
- **Check for HOA/COA arrears** — these can survive foreclosure
- **Verify property taxes** — outstanding tax certificates are not eliminated
- **Drive by the property** — you cannot access the interior before sale
- **Estimate rehab costs**

### Step 3: Place Your Bid
On auction day, proxy bids placed earlier compete automatically. The plaintiff typically opens bidding at or near the judgment amount.

### Step 4: Pay the Balance
**Florida judicial sales require cash payment.** If you are the winning bidder:
- Pay the full remaining balance by **close of business on auction day**
- Payment must be in certified funds, cashier's check, or wire transfer
- If you fail to pay, you forfeit your deposit

---

## After the Auction: Certificate of Title

After winning and paying, the Clerk issues a **Certificate of Sale**. A 10-day window follows for objections. Then the **Certificate of Title** — your formal ownership document — is issued.

**Surplus funds:** If your winning bid exceeds the total judgment amount, the surplus is deposited with the Clerk for the prior owner to claim.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a real estate license to bid at Palm Beach County foreclosure auctions?**
No. Any individual or entity can register and bid.

**Can I finance a foreclosure auction purchase?**
No. Florida judicial sales require full cash payment by end of business day.

**What is a Certificate of Title vs. a Certificate of Sale?**
The Certificate of Sale is issued immediately after auction. The Certificate of Title is issued after the 10-day objection period — it actually transfers legal ownership.
