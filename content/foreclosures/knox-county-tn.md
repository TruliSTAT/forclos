## Tennessee's Non-Judicial Foreclosure System

Tennessee is a **deed of trust state**, which means most home loans are secured by a deed of trust rather than a traditional mortgage. That distinction matters enormously when a borrower defaults: under **Tennessee Code Annotated (TCA) Title 35, Chapter 5**, lenders can foreclose using a **power-of-sale clause** embedded in the deed of trust — no lawsuit, no judge, no lengthy courtroom proceedings.

This non-judicial process makes Tennessee foreclosures significantly faster than judicial states. From the first missed payment to auction day, the typical timeline runs **60 to 90 days**, though lenders often allow more time before initiating the sale.

### How Power-of-Sale Works

When a borrower defaults, the lender instructs the trustee named in the deed of trust to advertise and conduct the sale. **Key statutory requirements (TCA §35-5-101):**

- **Publication notice:** The sale must be advertised in a local newspaper of general circulation for **three consecutive weeks**, providing at least **20 days of public notice** before the auction date.
- **Notice to borrower:** The borrower receives written notice of the sale date, location, and terms.
- **Public sale:** The auction is typically conducted at the Knox County Courthouse or another publicly designated location in Knoxville.

Because the process bypasses the court system, there is **no statutory right of redemption** after a non-judicial foreclosure sale in Tennessee.

## Step-by-Step Guide to Buying Knox County TN Foreclosure Listings

### Step 1 — Find the Listings

- **Knox County Register of Deeds** (rod.knoxcounty.org): Search recorded substitute trustee appointments and notices of foreclosure sale.
- **Knox County Trustee** (trustee.knoxcounty.org): View the official tax sale property list.
- **Legal notices** in the *Knoxville News Sentinel*: Required publication for non-judicial sales.

### Step 2 — Research Each Property

Foreclosure properties are sold **as-is**. Before bidding:

1. Order a preliminary title search through the Knox County Register of Deeds to identify superior liens, IRS liens, and HOA claims.
2. Drive the property; inspect the exterior. Interior access is rarely granted before sale.
3. Check Knox County Property Assessor records for assessed value, tax history, and square footage.
4. Verify whether any federal tax liens exist — IRS liens carry a **120-day redemption right** post-sale.

### Step 3 — Register and Arrange Financing

For **lender trustee sales**, bring cashier's checks or certified funds — personal checks and wire transfers are not accepted at the auction site.

For **Knox County tax sales**, bidder eligibility has a strict requirement: **all bidders must be fully current on Knox County property taxes.** Pre-registration is required.

### Step 4 — Attend the Auction

For tax sales at The Grande Event Center, doors open at 8:00 AM with bidding beginning at 10:00 AM. Bring:

- Valid government-issued photo ID
- Proof of pre-registration (tax sales)
- Certified funds or cashier's check
- Confirmation that you are current on all Knox County property taxes

### Step 5 — Pay and Close

**Knox County Tax Sale payment options:**

| Option | Details |
|---|---|
| Full payment at sale | Cash, certified funds, cashier's check, money order, or ACH |
| Deposit + promissory note | Minimum bid amount PLUS 30% of winning bid; note must be paid in full within 10 days |
| Personal/business checks | **Not accepted** |
| Wire transfers | **Not accepted** |

### Step 6 — Receive the Deed

After full payment:
- **Tax sales:** The Knox County Chancery Court issues a **Clerk and Master's Deed** after expiration of the applicable redemption period.
- **Trustee sales:** The trustee records a **Trustee's Deed** with the Knox County Register of Deeds. Title transfers immediately.

## Post-Sale Rights and Risks

### No Statutory Redemption for Non-Judicial Sales

Tennessee **eliminated the right of redemption** for non-judicial (deed of trust) foreclosures. Once the trustee's sale closes and the deed records at the Knox County Register of Deeds, the former owner has no legal right to repurchase the property.

### IRS Lien Exception

If the IRS holds a tax lien against the property, federal law grants them a **120-day right of redemption** after a non-judicial foreclosure sale.

### Occupancy and Possession

Properties sold at foreclosure auction may be occupied by former owners or tenants. Tennessee law requires that new owners pursue lawful eviction through the court system.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**1. Where do Knox County TN foreclosure auctions take place?**
Lender (trustee) sales typically occur at or near the Knox County Courthouse at City-County Building, 400 Main Street, Knoxville. Knox County delinquent tax sales are held at announced venues — most recently The Grande Event Center, 5441 Clinton Hwy., Knoxville.

**2. How much notice is required before a Tennessee foreclosure sale?**
Tennessee law (TCA §35-5-101) requires at least **20 days of published notice** via three consecutive weekly advertisements in a newspaper of general circulation in Knox County.

**3. Can the former owner take back the property after a Knox County foreclosure auction?**
For non-judicial (power-of-sale) foreclosures, **no** — Tennessee eliminated the post-sale statutory right of redemption. Note the IRS exception for federal tax liens (120-day federal redemption right).

**4. Do I need a real estate agent or attorney to buy a foreclosure in Knox County?**
Neither is legally required. However, given the as-is purchase conditions and title risks, many buyers engage a Tennessee real estate attorney for title examination and closing.

**5. What happens to properties that don't sell at the Knox County Tax Sale?**
Unsold tax sale properties become eligible for **Rebid** — only properties that did not sell on the auction day qualify. Contact the Knox County Chancery Court for the current Rebid list.
