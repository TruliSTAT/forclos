# Clark County, NV Foreclosure Auction Guide — Las Vegas Area 2026

Clark County is one of the most active foreclosure markets in the American West — and one of the fastest. Nevada's nonjudicial foreclosure law gives lenders the authority to complete a trustee sale in approximately 111 days from Notice of Default to sale. Compare that to California's 4+ months or Florida's 18+ month judicial process, and you understand why investors from across the country pay attention to Clark County.

This guide covers all three foreclosure tracks operating in Clark County, explains how to participate in county-run auctions, and walks you through the due diligence required to bid smart in the Las Vegas metro market.

---

## How Clark County Foreclosures Work

Clark County — which encompasses Las Vegas, Henderson, North Las Vegas, and surrounding communities — operates under Nevada's nonjudicial foreclosure framework, governed primarily by **NRS 107** (Nevada's Deed of Trust Act). Most residential mortgages in Nevada use a deed of trust rather than a mortgage, enabling lenders to foreclose without court involvement.

Three separate foreclosure tracks operate in Clark County:

1. **Lender-Driven Trustee Sales** (nonjudicial, NRS 107) — The primary foreclosure mechanism
2. **County Tax Lien Auctions** (treasurer.clarkcountynv.gov) — For properties with delinquent property taxes
3. **SID Lien Sales** — Special Improvement District assessments unique to Nevada HOA communities

Most foreclosure guides cover only the first track. Clark County's SID lien sales represent a distinct and often overlooked avenue for investors.

## The Three Tracks: Trustee Sales, Tax Liens & SID Sales

### Track 1: Lender-Driven Trustee Sales (NRS 107)

Nevada's nonjudicial timeline is among the shortest in the country:

| Step | Timeframe |
|------|-----------|
| Borrower default | Day 0 |
| Notice of Default filed | After 35 days |
| Mandatory waiting period | 90 days from NOD |
| Notice of Trustee Sale posted | After 90-day period |
| Trustee Sale | 21 days after NTS posting |
| **Total timeline** | **~111 days from NOD** |

Under NRS 107.080, the trustee sale must be conducted as a public auction. The lender opens bidding at the outstanding loan balance plus fees. Buyers must have cash-equivalent funds available on sale day.

**Homeowner protections:** Nevada's 2009-era legislation (AB 149 provisions, now codified) requires specific default notice procedures. Owner-occupants should consult with a Nevada attorney about any potential defenses prior to sale.

### Track 2: Clark County Tax Lien Auction

The Clark County Treasurer conducts periodic tax lien auctions for properties with delinquent property taxes. The official auction portal is **treasurer.clarkcountynv.gov/auction**.

- **Auction schedule:** Clark County holds auction cycles periodically — check the official portal for the current schedule and next scheduled auction date
- **Publication:** The county publishes auction lists on Thursdays via the AuctionList page
- **Opening bids:** Begin at the delinquent tax amount plus interest and fees
- **Registration:** Required in advance through the county's portal

⚠️ **Important:** The most recent scheduled tentative auction date has passed. Do not rely on specific past dates — always verify the current schedule at treasurer.clarkcountynv.gov/auction before taking any action.

### Track 3: Special Improvement District (SID) Lien Sales

This is Clark County's unique third track. Nevada HOA communities — particularly in master-planned developments across Henderson, Summerlin, and Las Vegas — often include SID (Special Improvement District) assessments. These are government-assessed liens for infrastructure (roads, utilities) within planned communities.

When SID assessments go delinquent, the county can sell those liens separately from the property itself, or in conjunction with a tax sale.

- **Authority:** clark.legistar.com — SID Sale Resolution Jan 29 2026 confirmed active
- **Who this affects:** Property owners in SID-designated communities (common in Southwest/Southern Highlands/Henderson areas)
- **Why it matters for buyers:** A property purchased at trustee sale may carry a surviving SID lien if not identified in due diligence

Clark County's SID lien structure is one of the most Nevada-specific aspects of its foreclosure market — and one most out-of-state investors miss.

## Clark County Tax Lien Auction — How to Participate

**Step 1: Find properties**  
The Treasurer publishes lists of eligible properties at treasurer.clarkcountynv.gov/auction. Review each property in the Clark County Assessor's database (assessor.clarkcountynv.gov) for parcel data, zoning, and assessed value.

**Step 2: Check for SID liens**  
For any property in a master-planned community, research whether it falls within a Special Improvement District. Contact the Clark County Treasurer's office if unclear.

**Step 3: Register**  
Complete the county's pre-registration process. Deposits are required before bidding.

**Step 4: Due diligence**  
Tax lien auction properties are sold as-is. You cannot inspect the interior of occupied properties. Drive-by inspections, assessor records, and any available code violation history are your primary research tools.

**Step 5: Bid and close**  
Winning bidders complete payment per the county's process. The county issues a deed — but title research is essential before bidding, as federal tax liens (IRS) may survive the sale under the 120-day rule.

## Bidding at Clark County Auctions — Requirements & Process

**At trustee sales:**
- Full payment due at sale in cash-equivalent form (cashier's check payable to trustee)
- No financing contingencies; no due diligence period after bidding
- Research before you bid — not after

**At county tax auctions:**
- Pre-fund your account through the county's portal before auction day
- Read the county's terms carefully — Clark County rules differ from RealAuction-platform counties

**Financial reality check:** At the Las Vegas metro's current price tier, most foreclosure purchases require cash or hard-money financing. Conventional lenders generally won't fund at trustee sales.

## Redemption Rights in Nevada

Nevada **does not** provide a post-sale redemption right for nonjudicial trustee sales under NRS 107. Once the trustee sale is held and the deed recorded, the transaction is final. The prior owner cannot redeem the property.

For tax lien sales, Nevada similarly does not provide post-sale redemption for completed county tax auctions.

This makes Clark County's nonjudicial market one of the cleanest in the country for buyers — no year-long redemption uncertainty after purchase.

**Note for homeowners facing foreclosure:** NRS 107 does include notice requirements and a cure period before sale. Homeowners should consult a Nevada attorney about available options, including loan modification and Nevada's homeowner assistance resources.

## Special Improvement District (SID) Liens — The Hidden Angle

Clark County investors consistently overlook SID liens — and it can be expensive.

SID assessments fund infrastructure in Nevada's many master-planned communities (roads, street lighting, water systems). When the original developer defaulted on SID bonds during the 2008-2012 housing crisis, thousands of Clark County properties accumulated SID liens. While the crisis is past, SID-designated communities still exist throughout the county, and new SID-adjacent assessments continue.

**Before bidding on any Clark County property:**
- Search the Clark County Assessor (assessor.clarkcountynv.gov) for the parcel's SID designation
- Confirm with the Clark County Treasurer's office whether any SID amounts are outstanding
- Note that SID liens may survive trustee sales depending on their seniority

This research takes 30 minutes and can prevent a multi-thousand-dollar surprise after purchase.

## Common Mistakes Las Vegas Area Buyers Make

**Counting only on Zillow.** Zillow's 62 homes is a narrow MLS slice. The actual Clark County pipeline is substantially larger. Use auction.com, the county auction portal, and foreclosure.com to see the full picture.

**Missing SID liens.** The Las Vegas metro is unique in its SID density. A missed SID lien can add thousands to your ownership cost.

**Assuming the schedule is current.** Clark County auction dates change. Always verify at treasurer.clarkcountynv.gov/auction before making any travel or financial plans.

**Skipping title research.** Nevada's fast timeline creates pressure to move quickly — but IRS federal liens filed within 120 days of sale may survive. Get a preliminary title report.

**Underestimating Nevada rehab costs.** Las Vegas construction costs have risen significantly since 2020. Factor in realistic renovation budgets before calculating returns.

**Bidding on occupied properties without an eviction plan.** Nevada's eviction process takes time. If the property is occupied, factor in the 30-90 day eviction timeline and associated legal costs.

## FAQ

**How fast is Nevada's foreclosure process?**  
Nevada's nonjudicial process under NRS 107 takes approximately 111 days from Notice of Default to trustee sale — among the fastest in the country. This speed benefits lenders and creates rapid investment opportunities.

**Where do I find Clark County foreclosure auction listings?**  
The official county tax auction portal is treasurer.clarkcountynv.gov/auction. For trustee sales and broader inventory, check auction.com, foreclosure.com, and the county recorder's office for posted Notices of Trustee Sale.

**What is a SID lien and does it affect foreclosure properties?**  
A Special Improvement District lien is a government-assessed infrastructure charge on properties in master-planned Nevada communities. SID liens may survive a trustee sale if they hold senior lien position. Always research SID status at the Clark County Assessor before bidding.

**Can I get a mortgage to buy at a foreclosure auction?**  
Generally no. Trustee sales and county tax auctions require cash-equivalent payment. Bank-owned (REO) properties listed on the MLS after foreclosure can typically be purchased with conventional financing.

**Does Nevada have a redemption period?**  
No. Nevada does not provide post-sale redemption rights for completed nonjudicial trustee sales or county tax auction sales.

**What's the difference between the Clark County tax auction and a trustee sale?**  
A trustee sale is initiated by the mortgage lender when a borrower defaults on their loan. A county tax auction is initiated by the county when property taxes go unpaid. They are separate processes, governed by separate statutes, and operate through different portals.
