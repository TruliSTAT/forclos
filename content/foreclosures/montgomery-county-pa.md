## How Foreclosure Works in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania is a **judicial foreclosure state**, meaning a lender cannot simply repossess a home when a borrower stops paying. Instead, the lender must file a lawsuit in the Court of Common Pleas, serve the homeowner, and obtain a court judgment before any sheriff sale can take place. This process provides meaningful protections for homeowners — and a predictable pipeline of distressed properties for investors.

### The PA Foreclosure Timeline

| Stage | Approximate Timeframe |
|---|---|
| First missed mortgage payment | Day 1 |
| Act 91 Notice sent to borrower | ~30–60 days |
| Lender files complaint in court | ~90–180 days |
| Homeowner served, response period | 20–30 days |
| Court enters judgment | Varies; often 6–12 months |
| Sheriff Sale scheduled | 4–8 weeks post-judgment |
| **Total: first missed payment to auction** | **~12–18 months** |

Two key Pennsylvania statutes govern this timeline:

- **Act 91 of 1983** — Requires lenders to send formal notice and connect borrowers with housing counselors before filing suit on a residential mortgage.
- **Act 6 of 1974** — Imposes additional notice requirements and limits attorney fees the lender can recover, providing further homeowner protections.

Because of these requirements, Pennsylvania's average foreclosure timeline is longer than most states — which means the properties appearing in Montgomery County PA foreclosure listings have typically been in the pipeline for more than a year.

## Step-by-Step Guide to Bidding at a Montgomery County Sheriff Sale

### Step 1 — Register on Bid4Assets

Create a free account at bid4assets.com. Registration is required to bid on any Montgomery County sheriff sale property. Confirm your email and complete your profile before the auction you plan to participate in.

### Step 2 — Submit Your Deposit

All bidders must submit a deposit **before** the auction closes. Key deposit rules:

- **One deposit** covers all properties in a single auction date
- Accepted payment methods: **certified check, money order, or wire transfer** — ACH, direct deposit, credit card, and cash app transfers are **not** accepted
- Allow up to **48 business hours** for deposit clearance; certified checks may take an additional business day
- Submit early — late deposits are not guaranteed

### Step 3 — Conduct Due Diligence

All properties are sold **AS IS**, with no expressed or implied warranties. The sheriff and Bid4Assets are not liable for any defects, liens, or title issues. Before bidding on any Montgomery County PA foreclosure listing, you should:

- Research outstanding municipal liens (sewer, water, trash) — these often survive the sale
- Check for IRS or state tax liens, which may also survive
- Review the title commitment or do a title search
- Drive by the property to assess physical condition
- Consult the Montgomery County Due Diligence guide: montgomerycountypa.gov/DocumentCenter/View/42719

### Step 4 — Place Your Bids

Bid4Assets uses a **timed, online auction format**. Auctions close at staggered times on the sale day. You may bid on as many properties as you like with a single deposit. There is no maximum bid limit imposed by your deposit amount.

### Step 5 — Settle the Purchase

Winning bidders must complete full payment within **12 calendar days** of the auction close. There are no exceptions. Payment must be received by Bid4Assets before the deadline, or:

- Your deposit is **forfeited** to the Montgomery County Sheriff's Office
- You may be **banned** from future Bid4Assets auctions in the county

A **$35 per-parcel administrative fee** is added to every winning bid.

## Post-Sale Rights: What Happens After You Win?

### Deed Transfer

Once full payment is received and recorded, the Montgomery County Sheriff's Office issues a **Sheriff's Deed** transferring title to the winning bidder. This deed is generally free of the foreclosed mortgage, but other encumbrances may survive.

### Redemption Rights

Unlike some states, **Pennsylvania does not grant homeowners a post-sale statutory redemption period** for mortgage foreclosures. Once the sheriff sale is confirmed and the deed is delivered, the former owner has no right to reclaim the property by repaying the debt. This is a key advantage for buyers in Montgomery County PA foreclosure listings compared to markets in redemption-period states.

### Occupied Properties

Be aware that some foreclosed properties are still occupied at the time of sale. As the new owner, you are responsible for any eviction proceedings. Pennsylvania law governs the eviction process — consult an attorney if you win a property with occupants.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need to be physically present to bid at a Montgomery County sheriff sale?**
A: No. All sales are fully online through Bid4Assets. You can bid from anywhere with an internet connection, as long as your deposit is on file and your account is verified before the auction closes.

**Q: Can I inspect a property before bidding?**
A: The sheriff's office does not facilitate interior inspections. Properties are sold AS IS. You may drive by or inspect the exterior, but interior access before the sale is not guaranteed. Winning bidders gain access after the deed is recorded.

**Q: What liens survive a Montgomery County sheriff sale?**
A: Generally, the foreclosed mortgage is extinguished. However, municipal liens (sewer, water, trash), IRS tax liens (with a 120-day federal redemption period), and certain other encumbrances may survive. Always conduct a title search before bidding.

**Q: How do I find out what properties are coming up?**
A: Browse current Montgomery County PA foreclosure listings at bid4assets.com/MontcoPASheriff or salesweb.civilview.com. The county also publishes a Sale Day Report one week before each auction at montgomerycountypa.gov/1374.

**Q: What happens if a property is postponed or removed from the sale?**
A: Cases can be postponed or withdrawn at any time before or during the sale, often due to bankruptcy filings, loan reinstatement, or settlement between the lender and borrower. Check the Sale Day Report the morning of the auction for last-minute changes.

## Montgomery County Real Estate Market Context

Montgomery County is the third most populous county in Pennsylvania, with over 850,000 residents across its 62 municipalities. The county borders Philadelphia to the south and stretches northwest through communities like Conshohocken, Blue Bell, Horsham, Hatboro, Pottstown, and Lansdale. Median home values in Montgomery County consistently outpace the Pennsylvania state average, making foreclosure listings an attractive entry point for both investors and owner-occupants seeking below-market properties.

Typical montgomery county pa foreclosure listings span a wide range of property types — from rowhouses in Norristown and Pottstown to single-family colonials in the Main Line corridor. Because PA's judicial process is thorough, properties that reach the sheriff sale stage have typically exhausted other resolution options, meaning motivated buying opportunities are real — but so is the need for careful due diligence.
